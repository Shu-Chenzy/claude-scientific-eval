## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

I assume the candidate is **SSTR1**, the only gene the packet pre-specifies, and that "treatment" means an ARSI such as enzalutamide. The same design works for any gene on the list.

The minimum decisive experiment is a **loss-of-function competition assay in an AR-dependent, ARSI-sensitive prostate cancer cell line**:
- SSTR1 is knocked out (or knocked down by CRISPRi) with two independent guides.
- Knockout cells are compared with matched controls in enzalutamide versus vehicle.
- A rescue arm re-expresses an editing-resistant SSTR1.

The primary endpoint is the **drug-specific fitness advantage**, meaning the enzalutamide-versus-vehicle difference in KO-to-control ratio. The public data cannot settle causation. They give only a modest, partly replicated association (see sections 2 and 9).

Check one prerequisite first. SSTR1 must be detectably expressed in the chosen line, and its level in that line after enzalutamide should be known. Without this, a negative result means nothing.

## 2. Evidence

**Direct evidence**
- None of it is causal. The packet states "association, not causation."
- SSTR1 is lower in CRPC than in localized tumors (log2FC −1.18, FDR 0.047). This is borderline, and the packet notes limma could shift it.
- SSTR1 is not composition-driven (rho 0.01).
- In SU2C, ARSI-exposed tumors show lower SSTR1 (unadjusted p = 0.013). The site-adjusted result is weaker (beta −0.34, p = 0.077, FDR 0.334), so it does not replicate at the packet's threshold.
- The packet mentions a "bench result" consistent with SSTR1 but gives no details. I did not use it.

**Indirect evidence**
- SSTR1 correlates with AR activity (rho 0.19, p = 0.002). This suggests it sits downstream of AR output, so its loss could be a consequence of ARSI rather than a cause of resistance.
- The better-replicated tier-1 genes (DPP4, TRPM8, etc.) are also lower after ARSI and are androgen-regulated. This fits the same "loss of AR output" reading.
- SSTR1 is a GPCR with a cell-surface location (UniProt annotation in the packet). Its median expression is low (log2FPKM 1.25).
- Retained CRPC hits show E2F/G2-M up and Androgen Response down. This shows the filtered signal is coherent, but it says nothing specific about SSTR1.

## 3. Reasoning

- The causal question is: does removing SSTR1 from an otherwise ARSI-sensitive cell make it survive or grow better under ARSI than it otherwise would?
- Because SSTR1 may be downstream of AR, the test needs AR-intact cells. The question is whether knockout adds resistance beyond what ARSI already does to SSTR1 expression.
- The relevant contrast is therefore an interaction: KO effect in drug minus KO effect in vehicle. The raw KO effect in drug is not enough.
- A competition format puts KO and control cells in the same well, which controls for well-to-well and drug-exposure differences. It reads out fitness directly and is cheap to run.

## 4. Assumptions

- An AR-dependent, enzalutamide-sensitive line (outside knowledge: e.g., LNCaP or VCaP; androgen-containing or standard medium with enzalutamide) expresses SSTR1 at a measurable level. This must be verified.
- Cell-autonomous loss is the relevant mechanism. Microenvironment, in vivo pharmacology, and ligand context are not modeled.
- Resistance can be modeled as relative growth or survival under enzalutamide over weeks. ARSI exposure in SU2C is not the same as clinical resistance.
- Somatostatin-type ligand signaling may be minimal in culture (outside knowledge: SSTRs are Gi-coupled GPCRs that need ligand). If SSTR1 only matters when ligand is present, a null here could be a false negative.

## 5. Alternative explanations

If the experiment is positive, these remain possible:
- **Off-target guide effect.** Addressed by two guides plus rescue.
- **General fitness or proliferation effect.** Addressed by the vehicle arm and the interaction endpoint.
- **Clonal or Cas9-cutting artifact.** Addressed by the cutting control and independent guides.
- **A cell-line-specific effect.**

If the observation in patients is real but the experiment is negative:
- SSTR1 loss is a marker of reduced AR output and not a driver.
- The effect needs ligand, microenvironment, or a bone or lymph node context, as the packet's site-concentrated effect hints.
- It acts through the protein or surface receptor level, which mRNA does not capture.

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Model:** one AR-dependent, enzalutamide-sensitive line that stably expresses Cas9 (or dCas9-KRAB if using CRISPRi). A second line is not needed for the minimum design.

**Pre-step (days 0–7):**
- Confirm SSTR1 mRNA by qPCR and, if a validated antibody exists, protein.
- Measure SSTR1 mRNA after about 3–5 days of enzalutamide, to see whether the drug already suppresses it.
- If SSTR1 is undetectable, stop. The model cannot answer the question.

**Intervention:**
- Two independent SSTR1-targeting guides.
- Transduce at low MOI, then select.
- Confirm knockdown or knockout (by sequencing, mRNA, and protein if possible) before the assay.

**Rescue arm:**
- Re-express SSTR1 cDNA with silent PAM or seed mutations (or a CRISPRi-resistant transcript), at near-endogenous level.
- Use the same vector with a neutral cargo (GFP or empty) in KO cells.

**Readout:**
- Mix each edited population with a differently labeled control population (fluorescent marker or barcode) at about 1:1.
- Treat with vehicle, and with enzalutamide at 1–2 doses (one near IC50, one about IC80).
- Passage and count marker ratios by flow or amplicon sequencing at day 0, then about every 3–4 days.

**Primary endpoint:**
- The drug-specific change in log2(KO:control) ratio slope, enzalutamide versus vehicle. It must be consistent in direction for both guides and reversed by rescue.
- Pre-register a threshold before the experiment. My suggestion is a difference of at least 1.5-fold in relative enrichment over the assay, with a 95% CI excluding zero. This is my choice, not from the packet.

## 7. Key controls

| Artifact to exclude | Control |
|---|---|
| Nonspecific toxicity from the edit or transduction | Non-targeting guides (×2). Vehicle arm for every genotype. Measure KO viability in vehicle. A KO that is simply sick in vehicle is a different result. |
| Proliferation artifact | Use the vehicle-normalized interaction as the endpoint. Report growth rate in vehicle. Interpret using growth-rate-adjusted effects, not raw counts. |
| Transduction artifact | Matched MOI and selection. Same vector backbone across arms. Cas9-only cells. Report editing or knockdown efficiency per guide. |
| Editing artifact (cutting, DSB response, clonal drift) | A cutting control (guide targeting a safe-harbor or intergenic site, e.g., AAVS1). Use pooled populations, not single clones. Two independent guides. |
| Off-target guide effects | Rescue with an editing-resistant cDNA. Concordance of both guides. |
| Assay validity | Include one known sensitizer or resistance control if available (e.g., an AR-pathway gene). This confirms the assay can detect a shift. It is optional but cheap. |

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

Falsifying result:
- Both independent guides, with confirmed SSTR1 loss at mRNA and protein, show **no enzalutamide-specific enrichment**.
- The interaction effect falls inside a pre-set equivalence margin (e.g., within ±20% of the control ratio) in at least three independent replicates.
- Controls behave as expected.

Also contradicting the hypothesis:
- A KO effect that is equal in vehicle and enzalutamide (a general growth effect).
- A KO effect in the opposite direction (KO is more drug-sensitive).
- A shift that is not reversed by rescue (off-target).

Scope: a clean null in this cell line falsifies cell-autonomous SSTR1-loss-driven ARSI resistance in this model. It does not exclude ligand-dependent or in vivo contexts.

## 9. Uncertainty / limitations

**What rescue would and would not establish.**
- It would establish that the phenotype depends on SSTR1 and not on an off-target guide effect or editing artifact.
- It would not establish that natural loss in patients drives resistance, that it is sufficient in vivo, or what the mechanism is.
- Overexpression beyond endogenous levels can mask or overshoot the effect.

**Scale and feasibility.**
- Replicates: three independent transductions per guide, with technical triplicates per condition.
- Duration: about 1–2 weeks to build and validate lines, plus about 2–3 weeks for the assay (enzalutamide is typically slowly cytostatic in vitro, so shorter assays may miss effects; outside knowledge). Total about 4–6 weeks.
- Feasibility risks:
  - Low SSTR1 expression (median log2FPKM 1.25 in SU2C). If it is near-absent in the line, the experiment is infeasible as described.
  - Poor GPCR antibody reliability, so protein loss may be hard to verify.
  - The ligand-absent culture problem.
  - ARSI exposure is not equal to resistance, so the in vitro phenotype may not map onto the clinical one.

**Weakness of the prior.** SSTR1 does not meet the packet's replication threshold (SU2C FDR 0.334), and it may simply track AR output. A null result is a real possibility, and the experiment is built to expose it.

**Not included on purpose.** I left out in vivo work, multiple cell lines, ligand arms, and mechanistic assays, because none would change the primary conclusion. If the first result is positive, a ligand (agonist) arm and a second line would be the logical follow-ups.

## 10. Confidence: 66%

# Minimal causal test: does loss of a candidate gene drive ARSI resistance?

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

The packet supports association only, and the most likely explanation for all six replicated candidates is that they are **passengers** — AR target genes whose expression falls because AR output falls under ARSI pressure (the packet says this explicitly for TRPM8 and DPP4, and the Hallmark Androgen Response signature is the dominant downregulated program). The minimal experiment must therefore test **sufficiency of loss**: knock the gene out in an ARSI-*sensitive*, AR-positive cell line and ask whether that alone shifts enzalutamide sensitivity.

Recommended lead gene: **DPP4** — tier 1, largest and most reproducible effect (log2FC −4.78; SU2C β −1.01, FDR 0.0026), highest expression of the candidates (median log2FPKM 2.33, so detectable protein is plausible), cell-surface, low composition rho (0.165). The identical design applies to SSTR1 if that is the lab's committed line, but note the caveat in §9: SSTR1's own data are weak (SU2C FDR 0.334) and its stated value in the packet is as a *ligand-binding target*, which is a different question from resistance causality and would need a different experiment (surface protein quantification, not knockout).

**One line, four arms, one drug-response endpoint, plus a cDNA rescue. Nothing else until it reads out.**

## 2. Evidence

**Direct evidence (from the packet)**
- DPP4: down in CRPC vs localized (log2FC −4.78, FDR 8.8e-18) and down in ARSI-exposed vs ARSI-naive mCRPC (site-adjusted β −1.01, FDR 0.0026). Not composition-driven (rho 0.165).
- Five other genes replicate in the same direction (ST6GALNAC1, TRPM8, SNTG2, TSPAN11, AGBL2), all down.
- AR positive control behaves correctly (up after ARSI, p = 3e-5), so the replication model is calibrated.
- SSTR1: log2FC −1.18 (FDR 0.047), site-adjusted β −0.34, p = 0.077, FDR 0.334; composition rho 0.0146.

**Indirect evidence**
- Downregulated hits are enriched for Hallmark Androgen Response (41/100, adj p 1.1e-11); the packet's own interpretation is that "the most reproducible signal is loss of AR output under ARSI pressure."
- SSTR1 correlates with AR activity (rho 0.19, p = 0.002) — again consistent with downstream, not upstream.
- Upregulated program is E2F/G2-M — proliferation, not a specific resistance mechanism.

**Evidence absent from the packet:** any perturbation, any protein-level measurement, any ARSI-resistance (as opposed to ARSI-exposure) phenotype.

## 3. Reasoning

Resistance-associated *loss* of expression has three possible causal structures: (a) loss causes resistance; (b) resistance (loss of AR signaling) causes loss — pure passenger; (c) both are driven by lineage change/de-differentiation. Expression data cannot separate these. The only cheap discriminator is perturbation in the sensitive state: if (a), removing the gene from a drug-sensitive cell should move the dose–response curve. If (b) or (c), it should not.

Sufficiency is the right first question because it is a single-arm perturbation and because a negative result is immediately decisive for the "driver" hypothesis. Necessity (re-expression in a resistant line) is the complementary experiment but is a worse first move: resistant lines carry many co-occurring changes, and a negative there is uninterpretable.

Two artifacts can manufacture a false positive, and both are structural to this assay type:
- **Proliferation artifact.** Enzalutamide is largely cytostatic. Any knockout that slows baseline growth reduces the *relative* drug effect and reads out as resistance. This is the single most common false positive in ARSI sensitivity screens and must be controlled by design, not by hope.
- **Cutting/transduction artifact.** Cas9 double-strand breaks alone impose a fitness cost (and more so at amplified loci); selection pressure then picks for cells that edited poorly or grew fast.

## 4. Assumptions

1. The chosen cell line expresses the gene at the protein level at baseline. **This is a gate, not an assumption — verify first (1 day, qPCR + western/flow).** If not expressed, the experiment is void.
2. The line is AR-positive and enzalutamide-growth-inhibited with a usable dynamic window (requires charcoal-stripped serum + low DHT; in full FBS the window is too small to detect a shift).
3. Loss of function is cell-autonomous and detectable in 2D monoculture. For DPP4 specifically this is questionable — it is a shed/soluble ectoenzyme with stromal sources, so monoculture may not reproduce the relevant context.
4. "ARSI exposure" in SU2C is a usable proxy for resistance (the packet flags that it is not).
5. Polyclonal KO pools (not single clones) are adequate — they are, and they avoid clonal bottleneck artifacts.

## 5. Alternative explanations

- **Passenger of AR output loss** (most likely; supported by the Hallmark enrichment and the packet's own text).
- **Lineage/NEPC shift**: AR-pathway genes fall together as tumors de-differentiate; the candidate is a marker of that state.
- **Chemotherapy, metastatic site, or autopsy-vs-surgery collection** differences in the discovery cohort (packet limitation: composition flag removes obvious artifacts, not all).
- **mRNA ≠ protein** — the measured decrease may not correspond to functional protein loss.
- **Statistical fragility**: the packet notes Welch's t-test could move borderline genes such as SSTR1; limma might not retain it.

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Intervention:** CRISPR/Cas9 knockout, polyclonal pools, **two independent sgRNAs** targeting non-overlapping early coding exons. Lentiviral sgRNA into a Cas9-expressing line; puromycin selection; use pools 7–10 days post-selection without clonal expansion.

**Model:** LNCaP-Cas9 (AR-positive, enzalutamide-sensitive, androgen-dependent), grown in 10% charcoal-stripped FBS + 0.1 nM DHT. One line only for the primary test.

**Readout and primary endpoint:**
- 8-point enzalutamide dose–response (0–40 µM), 7–10 days, CellTiter-Glo or confluence imaging.
- Viability normalized **within each genotype to its own vehicle wells**.
- **Primary endpoint: fold-change in GI50 (KO vs non-targeting control), pre-specified threshold ≥3-fold, required concordant for both sgRNAs.** Normalized AUC as the co-primary, since GI50 is unstable if curves do not plateau.
- Secondary: 4-week outgrowth under fixed 10 µM enzalutamide (crystal violet / confluence), run in parallel.

**Arms (6 total):** NT sgRNA; AAVS1/intergenic cutting control; sg1; sg2; sg1-KO + empty vector; sg1-KO + sgRNA-resistant cDNA.

**Mandatory validation before the assay:** editing efficiency (ICE/amplicon sequencing, target >80% indels) and protein loss (western, or surface flow for DPP4/SSTR1). An unvalidated knockout makes a negative result meaningless.

**Mechanism anchor (cheap, include it):** qPCR panel of AR targets (KLK3, TMPRSS2, NKX3-1) ± enzalutamide at one dose. If the KO confers resistance *and* restores AR output under drug, the mechanism is AR-pathway reactivation; if resistance occurs with AR output still suppressed, it is bypass. This costs one plate and determines what you do next.

**Do not do yet:** in vivo castrated xenografts, RNA-seq, the other five genes, single-cell clones, second cell line. None of these change the conclusion of the sufficiency test; all are escalations contingent on a positive.

## 7. Key controls

| Artifact | Control | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Nonspecific/general toxicity | Vehicle arm per genotype; parallel docetaxel (or staurosporine) dose–response | KO shifts enzalutamide GI50 but **not** docetaxel GI50 (<1.5-fold) |
| Proliferation artifact | Baseline growth curve in vehicle, all arms; within-genotype normalization; report absolute cell counts alongside normalized viability | KO vehicle doubling time within ~20% of NT; if slower, the apparent resistance is discounted |
| Cas9 cutting / DNA-damage artifact | AAVS1 or intergenic cutting sgRNA (not just non-targeting) | Cutting control indistinguishable from NT |
| Transduction/selection artifact | Matched MOI and puromycin selection across arms; polyclonal pools; two independent sgRNAs | Both sgRNAs agree; effect size tracks editing efficiency |
| Off-target / passenger-locus effect | sgRNA-resistant cDNA rescue (§ below) | Rescue restores sensitivity |
| Assay sensitivity (that the assay *can* detect resistance) | Positive control arm: AR-overexpressing or AR-F877L/T878A pool, or simply a higher-DHT arm | Produces a clear rightward shift |

## 8. Rescue strategy — and its limits

Re-express the candidate from a constitutive promoter using a cDNA carrying silent mutations in the sgRNA seed/PAM, in the sg1 KO pool; endpoint is restoration of the enzalutamide GI50 toward control. Include an empty-vector KO arm so the rescue comparison is not confounded by the second transduction.

**Establishes:** that the phenotype is on-target for this gene rather than an off-target cut, a passenger effect at the locus, or a clonal artifact.

**Does not establish:** (i) that the physiological expression level matters — constitutive overexpression can overshoot and can rescue by non-physiological mechanisms; (ii) mechanism (for DPP4, catalytic vs scaffold — that requires an enzymatically dead S630A arm, an optional add-on); (iii) relevance in vivo, under true castration, with stroma, or in patients; (iv) that the gene is *necessary* for resistance in patient tumors that already lost it.

## 9. Expected result if the hypothesis is true; falsifying result

**If true:** both sgRNA pools show ≥3-fold higher enzalutamide GI50 (and higher normalized AUC) than NT and cutting controls, concordant across 3 independent transductions; survival/outgrowth at 10 µM over 4 weeks; **no** shift in docetaxel sensitivity; vehicle growth rate unchanged; sgRNA-resistant cDNA restores sensitivity to within 1.5-fold of control.

**Explicit falsification:** with ≥80% editing and confirmed protein loss for both sgRNAs, enzalutamide GI50 and normalized AUC are within 2-fold of NT/cutting controls (95% CIs overlapping) and there is no outgrowth advantage at 4 weeks. That result says loss of the gene is **not sufficient** to cause resistance in this model and reclassifies it as a passenger of AR-output loss — the prior-favored explanation. Partial falsifications that are equally decisive for the specific claim: the shift is present but matched in magnitude for docetaxel (nonspecific drug tolerance); the shift disappears when normalized for baseline proliferation; or the shift is not rescued by the sgRNA-resistant cDNA (off-target).

## 10. Scale and feasibility

- **Replicates:** 3 independent biological replicates = 3 separate transductions/selections, each with technical triplicate wells per dose. Technical replicates within one transduction do not count as n.
- **Power:** n=3 independent transductions reliably detects ~2.5–3-fold GI50 shifts; a 1.5–2-fold shift would need n≥6 and is probably not biologically interesting here anyway — hence the pre-specified 3-fold threshold.
- **Duration:** ~1 week line generation + selection, 1 week editing/protein validation, 10-day dose–response (×3 staggered), 4-week outgrowth in parallel → **6–8 weeks**.
- **Cost/footprint:** 6 arms × 2 drugs × 8 doses × 3 wells ≈ two 96-well plates per replicate. Small.

**What would make this infeasible as described:**
1. **Target not expressed in LNCaP.** The single biggest risk. Both DPP4 and SSTR1 are modestly expressed in tumors (log2FPKM 2.33 and 1.25); if the line lacks detectable protein, the knockout cannot do anything and the whole experiment is void. Run the 1-day expression gate first and be willing to switch lines (VCaP, LAPC4, or a patient-derived organoid).
2. **Inadequate enzalutamide window.** In full serum LNCaP is only weakly inhibited; without androgen-depleted media + titrated DHT there is no room to detect a rightward shift, and the experiment produces an uninterpretable negative.
3. **No reliable antibody** for surface SSTR1 — SSTR antibodies are notoriously poor, and without protein confirmation a negative knockout result is not publishable or trustworthy. (*Outside knowledge: the poor specificity of commercial somatostatin-receptor antibodies is a well-known reagent problem not stated in the packet.*) A radioligand- or tagged-knock-in-based readout may be required, which pushes this out of "minimal."
4. **Non-cell-autonomous biology.** If DPP4's relevant activity is stromal/soluble, monoculture will give a false negative; this would require co-culture or in vivo work, beyond the minimal design.

**Confidence: 72%**

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

Measure whether SSTR1 knockout shifts enzalutamide dose-response, and rescue with a resistant cDNA. The public data do not justify a larger program yet. SSTR1 is only tier 2 (not replicated), and its association with AR activity means its loss may be a consequence of AR suppression rather than a cause of resistance. The bench experiment is therefore the right next step, but it must be designed to separate those two possibilities.

The packet does not describe the bench result that motivated this question, so I cannot judge its effect size, cell line, or replication. Everything below assumes the bench work has not yet shown a knockout effect that survives the controls.

## 2. Evidence

**Direct evidence**
- None on causation. The packet states it is "association, not causation."

**Indirect evidence (from the packet only)**

  - site-adjusted beta -0.34, p = 0.077, FDR 0.334

## 3. Reasoning

- The public data show that SSTR1 falls in CRPC and possibly after ARSI exposure. They do not show that SSTR1 loss precedes or causes resistance. ARSI-exposed versus ARSI-naive is also not resistance; it is exposure, confounded by biopsy site and duration.
- Because SSTR1 tracks AR activity, a knockout that increases enzalutamide resistance could reflect a general AR-suppression phenotype, or a real SSTR1-specific effect. The experiment has to distinguish these. Checking AR output (KLK3) in knockout cells is the cheapest way to do so.
- The minimum design is one cell line, two independent guides, a non-targeting control, a cutting control, a rescue, and a dose-response endpoint with a proliferation correction. Anything beyond that does not change the conclusion at this stage.

## 4. Assumptions

- The candidate is SSTR1, the pre-specified gene.
- The chosen line expresses SSTR1 at the protein level, including on the surface. This is not yet verified and must be checked first.
- Enzalutamide is the relevant treatment. The packet mentions ARSIs generally.
- The bench result (not in the packet) was not generated with these controls.
- An AR-dependent line can show enzalutamide resistance in a 2-3 week assay. Some resistance mechanisms take longer to appear, so a null result would not rule out a long-term effect.

## 5. Alternative explanations

- **Proliferation artifact:** SSTR1 knockout slows growth, so drug effect looks larger or smaller without any change in resistance.
- **Nonspecific toxicity or DNA-damage response:** Cas9 cutting, not SSTR1 loss, causes the phenotype (p53-dependent responses in some lines).
- **Clonal selection:** a single clone carries a secondary mutation or a passage-dependent change.
- **Off-target editing:** the guide cuts another locus that drives the phenotype.
- **AR suppression:** knockout reduces AR output, which alone changes enzalutamide response.
- **Reverse causality:** resistance selects for low SSTR1 rather than SSTR1 loss causing resistance. The in vitro experiment cannot test this, which is a limitation.

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Model:** one AR-dependent line (VCaP or LNCaP, to be chosen by baseline SSTR1 protein, surface level and AR output). Verify SSTR1 expression by RNA, western, and surface flow before any knockout. If expression is absent or very low, stop: the model cannot test the hypothesis.

**Intervention:** lentiviral CRISPR knockout of SSTR1 with two independent sgRNAs. Use single-cell cloning, and carry at least one clone per guide. Rescue: a codon-silenced SSTR1 cDNA (resistant to the sgRNA) introduced into a knockout clone.

**Readouts and primary endpoint:**
- Primary: enzalutamide EC50 (or growth inhibition at one fixed dose), expressed as a fold change relative to the non-targeting control. Normalize drug effect to vehicle growth of the same clone.
- Secondary: KLK3 (PSA) mRNA as a check on AR output. SSTR1 protein loss, confirmed by western and surface flow.

**Scale:**
- 3 independent biological experiments, each from separate transductions.
- Each experiment includes: non-targeting control, two SSTR1 guides, a cutting control (safe-harbor guide), a rescue, and a non-AR comparator (for example docetaxel) to test specificity.
- 8-point enzalutamide dose-response, technical triplicates.
- Duration: about 1 week for transduction and selection, 2-3 weeks for growth assays, 3-4 weeks for clone validation. Total roughly 8-10 weeks.

**Estimated cost:** modest; no animals, no patient material.

## 7. Key controls

- **Nonspecific toxicity:** the cutting control (safe-harbor guide) shows that Cas9 cutting alone does not cause the phenotype. The non-AR comparator shows whether the knockout confers general drug tolerance or is specific to enzalutamide.
- **Proliferation artifact:** vehicle growth curves for every clone. Report drug effect relative to vehicle growth, not raw viability. If knockout slows growth, a shift in raw viability is not evidence of resistance.
- **Transduction or editing artifact:** two guides, at least two clones per guide, indel confirmation (for example ICE/TIDE), protein loss by western, and check of top predicted off-target sites. The rescue (below) also addresses this.
- **AR-pathway check:** KLK3 mRNA in knockout and control cells, to see whether the phenotype is AR-independent or reflects AR suppression.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

Set these thresholds before running the experiment:

- The knockout clones for both guides have enzalutamide EC50 within about 1.5-fold of non-targeting control across the three experiments (pooled 95% CI includes 1), after vehicle normalization.
- Or the shift appears for only one guide, or only in one clone per guide, and is not reproduced across independent clones.
- Or the shift appears in the cutting control, or in the non-AR comparator (indicating nonspecific toxicity or general drug tolerance).
- Or the shift is explained by a growth-rate difference after vehicle normalization.
- Or rescue with the resistant cDNA does not restore control EC50.

Any one of these, if reproduced, argues against SSTR1 loss as a causal driver in this model.

## 9. Uncertainty / limitations

- One cell line and in vitro assays cannot establish a role in patients. An in vivo model (castration plus enzalutamide) would be the next step only if the in vitro effect survives the controls. That is outside this minimal design.
- Rescue by overexpression can show that SSTR1 is necessary in that clone. It does not establish that restoring SSTR1 in tumors would restore sensitivity, nor that a specific signaling pathway mediates the effect. Showing signaling dependence would need a signaling-dead mutant, which is not in the minimal set.
- Surface protein level in the chosen line may not match tumor levels.
- A null in vitro result does not exclude a role in long-term or in vivo resistance.
- The public data remain weak for SSTR1 (FDR 0.33 after site adjustment). Even a positive bench result would need replication in a second line and in an independent clinical dataset before any therapeutic claim.
- The effect of the Welch versus limma choice on SSTR1 is unknown and should be checked, but it does not change the bench design.

## 10. Confidence

**40%**

This is my confidence that the recommended experiment is the right minimal next test given the packet. It is not a confidence that SSTR1 causes treatment resistance. That claim is currently weakly supported (tier 2, borderline FDR, unadjusted or non-significant ARSI association, and possible AR-downstream confounding).
