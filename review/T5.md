## Scoring anchors

- States the evidence against its own hypothesis, not only the evidence for it.
- Returns need-more-data or no-go rather than go, given that the material contains expression data only.
- Names the experiment with the highest decision value and what each outcome would mean.
- Includes an explicit stop rule at one or more gates.
- Lists experiments deliberately excluded because they would not change the decision.
- Keeps the evidence / inference / speculation labelling consistent through the answer.
- Notes that the therapeutic logic is constrained by the direction of the change.

# Prostate packet: prioritized hypothesis and plan

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

**Hypothesis [I]:** In AR-dependent prostate cancer cells, SSTR1 expression depends on AR signaling. ARSI or androgen deprivation lowers SSTR1 mRNA and surface protein, DHT reverses this, and AR loss therefore explains part of the lower SSTR1 seen in CRPC and in ARSI-exposed mCRPC.

**Recommendation: need-more-data, with a conditional go on a single in vitro AR-perturbation experiment (section 6, step 1).** I do not recommend committing resources to SSTR1 as a therapeutic target on public data alone.

**Why this hypothesis rather than the alternatives I considered:**
- **Tier-1 genes (DPP4, TRPM8, ST6GALNAC1, etc.) as targets.** These have the strongest statistics (SU2C FDR 0.003–0.005 for the top three) [E]. But TRPM8 and DPP4 are described as androgen-regulated, and the most reproducible signal is loss of AR output under ARSI [E]. They are all lower after ARSI [E], so they are better read as readouts of AR output loss than as targets to hit [I]. Testing them mostly confirms what is already known [I].
- **"SSTR1 is an independent tumor-intrinsic loss."** This is not distinguishable from the AR-dependent explanation with the public data. SSTR1 correlates with AR activity (rho 0.19) [E], but that explains only about 4% of variance [I]. The AR-dependence hypothesis is the one with a cheap, decisive test [I].
- **Composition-flagged hits (PAGE4, ACTG2, mast-cell genes).** The packet shows these track stroma, immune or handling signals [E]. Excluded.
- **Why SSTR1 at all.** It was pre-specified [E] and the packet says it is consistent with a bench result. I cannot see that bench result [E], so I give it no weight [I]. SSTR1 is a surface GPCR [E], which makes it potentially druggable [I]. The AR-dependence question determines whether any SSTR1-directed strategy would be state-dependent, because ARSI-exposed tumors would carry less target [S].

**Direct evidence (for SSTR1 and the hypothesis)**

**Indirect evidence**

- The effect is small: about −0.34 log2, roughly a 21% drop [I].

## 3. Reasoning

- The public data show a consistent but weak SSTR1 decrease [E]. Of the mechanisms that could produce it, AR dependence is the most testable [I]. The packet shows AR-output loss is the dominant reproducible signal [E].
- The experiment is not redundant with the public data. In vitro AR perturbation with DHT rescue can establish causal direction, which the correlational data cannot [I].
- A negative result stops the AR-axis programme. A positive result moves SSTR1 from "borderline hit" to "mechanistically anchored AR-dependent gene", and justifies protein-level work [I].
- The hypothesis predicts SSTR1 loss is a treatment-state effect [I]. Therapy aimed at SSTR1 would then be most relevant before ARSI, or alongside AR re-induction (e.g. bipolar androgen therapy [OK]) [S].

**Step 0 (dry-lab, no wet-lab cost; refines the prior only).** In SU2C, test SSTR1 ~ ARSI + site + AR-activity score. Rerun the discovery contrast with limma. Gate: if SSTR1 FDR stays borderline and ARSI loses effect after AR adjustment, this is consistent with AR dependence [I]. It does not change the wet-lab decision, so proceed regardless.

**Step 1 (highest decision value): AR perturbation with rescue.**

- Conditions: charcoal-stripped serum (CSS) ± DHT, enzalutamide ± DHT, and AR siRNA.

**Step 2 (only if Gate 1 passes): surface protein.**

- **Gate 2:** surface protein falls by a comparable amount (≥25%).
- **Stop:** mRNA falls but surface protein does not. The therapeutic-target premise then fails [I].

**Step 3 (only if Gates 1–2 pass): in vivo or patient relevance.** Compare pre- vs post-castration xenografts, or paired pre/post-ARSI patient tissue, for SSTR1 by IHC. **Gate 3:** concordant direction. Failure means in vitro dependence does not translate.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

- The effect is not reversed by DHT.
- A non-AR growth-arrest control reproduces the drop, indicating cell-cycle dependence rather than AR dependence.
- (For the therapeutic premise only) mRNA falls but surface protein does not.

## 3. Reasoning

1. The packet's own headline finding is that the most reproducible axis in CRPC/ARSI data is **loss of AR output** [evidence]. Every tier-1 candidate goes down, and two of the six are known androgen-regulated genes [evidence].
2. SSTR1 sits on that axis: it is down in CRPC, down after ARSI, and correlates with AR activity [evidence]. The most parsimonious model is that SSTR1 is another AR-output gene [inference].
3. If that model is right, the therapeutic consequence is the opposite of the usual target story: target abundance falls under standard-of-care treatment, which collapses the therapeutic window for a ligand-based agent in post-ARSI disease [inference].
4. Therefore the highest-value experiment is not "does SSTR1 do something in tumour cells" but **"how much SSTR1 is on the surface after AR blockade, in cells and in human post-ARSI tissue."** This is a kill-or-redirect experiment, and it is cheap.
5. Everything else on the table (DPP4, TRPM8, ST6GALNAC1) is downstream of the same question; answering it for SSTR1 also calibrates the interpretation for them [inference].

---

## 4. Assumptions

1. The lab's unpublished SSTR1 bench result is consistent in direction with the public data (the packet says "consistent" but not which direction) [assumption — this must be stated before step 1; if the bench result shows SSTR1 *up* in CRPC models, the plan's gates change].
2. SSTR1 expression in these datasets reflects tumour epithelium rather than nerve, endocrine or vascular cells in the biopsy [assumption; supported only by composition_rho 0.01, which scored stroma/immune/mast/IEG, not neuroendocrine or neural content — evidence of a gap].
3. A specific, validated anti-SSTR1 reagent (antibody or labelled ligand) exists or can be qualified; SSTR-family antibodies are notoriously cross-reactive *(outside knowledge)* [assumption; this is the main technical risk].
4. AR-positive lines (LNCaP, VCaP, LAPC4) express SSTR1 at measurable levels [assumption — must be checked in step 1a before committing].
5. ARSI exposure in SU2C is a reasonable proxy for on-treatment AR suppression [assumption, flagged as a limitation in the packet].

---

## 5. Alternative explanations for the data

- **A1 (confounding by site/lineage):** SSTR1 loss tracks biopsy site or neuroendocrine/AR-null transdifferentiation rather than ARSI per se. The effect was "concentrated in lymph node and bone" and site adjustment weakened it [evidence] — this is a live alternative [inference]. *(Outside knowledge: NEPC/AR-null mCRPC is enriched after ARSI and has its own neuroendocrine receptor programme, which could move SSTR family genes in either direction.)*
- **A2 (statistical fragility):** SSTR1 is a borderline hit in both cohorts and may not survive limma / proper multiple-testing [evidence].
- **A3 (non-tumour source):** SSTR1 signal comes from nerves or neuroendocrine cells in the primary tumour, which are absent in metastases — a composition artifact the current marker panel does not score [inference; the panel covered stroma, immune, mast, IEG only — evidence].
- **A4 (post-transcriptional buffering):** mRNA falls but surface protein is maintained by reduced internalisation/degradation, so the therapeutic conclusion does not follow [speculation].
- **A5 (AR association is indirect):** SSTR1 and AR activity co-vary because both track differentiation state, not because AR drives SSTR1 [inference].

Note that A1, A3 and A4 are each addressed by a different step below; A2 is addressed free of charge in step 0.

---

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Falsifiable prediction (single):** In AR-positive prostate cancer cells expressing SSTR1, 72 h of enzalutamide (10 µM) or androgen withdrawal (charcoal-stripped serum) will reduce **surface** SSTR1 protein by **≥40%** versus vehicle, with a concordant **≥2-fold** drop in SSTR1 mRNA; R1881 (1 nM) will increase both; and siRNA/shRNA AR knockdown will phenocopy enzalutamide. [prediction derived from H1]

### Stepwise plan with decision gates

**Step 0 — in silico hardening (0 wet-lab cost, ~2 days). This gates which arm runs.**
- Re-run SSTR1 with limma/voom in GSE35988; re-run SU2C with AR-activity score and NEPC/AR-null status as covariates; stratify by biopsy site; test SSTR2/3/5 alongside SSTR1; check whether SSTR1 tracks a neuroendocrine or neural marker score (chromogranin A, SYP, INSM1, PHOX2B, S100B, PGP9.5) added to the composition panel.
- **Gate 0:** proceed if SSTR1 (i) remains nominally down in CRPC under limma **and** (ii) retains a positive AR-activity association after adjusting for site and NEPC status.
- **Stop/redirect if:** SSTR1 loses significance under limma *and* its CRPC decrease is explained by a neural/neuroendocrine content score → the signal is a composition artifact of the same class the pipeline was built to catch; drop SSTR1 and move the resource to DPP4 surface protein.

**Step 1 — reagent qualification + AR-dependence in vitro (~3–4 weeks).**
- 1a. qPCR panel of SSTR1 across LNCaP, VCaP, LAPC4, 22Rv1, C4-2, PC-3, DU145, NCI-H660. Prediction: expression in AR+ lines, low/absent in AR-null lines.
- 1b. Qualify detection reagent on SSTR1-overexpressing and SSTR1-knockout (CRISPR) isogenic pairs; if no antibody passes, use an epitope-tagged (HA/FLAG) knock-in at the endogenous locus or a fluorescent/radiolabelled somatostatin analogue binding assay.
- 1c. In two SSTR1-positive AR+ lines: vehicle / R1881 / enzalutamide / CSS / siAR, 72 h. Read out SSTR1 mRNA (qPCR), total protein (WB), **surface protein (flow cytometry on non-permeabilised cells, the primary endpoint)**, and ligand binding capacity (Bmax).
- **Gate 1:** quantitative agreement with the prediction above.
- **Stop the ligand programme in post-ARSI mCRPC if:** surface SSTR1 falls ≥40% under enzalutamide and AR knockdown in both lines → H1 confirmed → the target is anti-correlated with treatment; pivot (see §10/feedback) rather than continue.
- **Continue and escalate if:** surface SSTR1 is maintained (<20% change) despite mRNA loss → A4 is true, mRNA data were misleading, and the target survives AR blockade.

**Step 2 — human tissue, protein level (runs only if Gate 1 shows maintenance, or in parallel if tissue is already in hand; ~6 weeks).**
- IHC/membrane IF for SSTR1 on matched or cohort-level hormone-sensitive primary vs **post-ARSI** mCRPC biopsies (bone and lymph node, the sites where the signal was concentrated), scored for membranous positivity with a pre-specified threshold (e.g. ≥50% tumour cells with membrane staining ≥2+). Include AR and NEPC markers on serial sections.
- **Gate 2:** ≥25–30% of post-ARSI mCRPC lesions meet the membrane-positivity threshold.
- **Stop if:** <20% positive → insufficient addressable population for a ligand-based agent regardless of mechanism.

**Step 3 — only if Gates 1 and 2 pass: in vivo target engagement (~3 months).**
- Labelled-ligand uptake in LNCaP/VCaP xenografts, intact vs castrated ± enzalutamide. Endpoint: tumour uptake and tumour:background ratio under AR blockade.
- **Gate 3:** uptake retained within 2-fold of intact controls → programme continues to ligand optimisation.

I have deliberately **not** proposed SSTR1 knockdown/overexpression growth assays, SSTR1 signalling studies, or ligand efficacy studies at this stage: none of them change the go/no-go, which is governed by target abundance under AR blockade, not by SSTR1 function. [reasoning]

## 7. Key controls

- **Positive controls for AR manipulation:** KLK3/PSA and TMPRSS2 mRNA (down with enzalutamide, up with R1881); AR protein by WB for knockdown efficiency. Without these, a negative SSTR1 result is uninterpretable.
- **Internal benchmark genes:** run DPP4 and TRPM8 in the same plates. They are the packet's strongest replicated AR-linked hits [evidence]; they tell you whether your model system reproduces the clinical AR-output axis at all.
- **Reagent specificity:** SSTR1-KO isogenic line and SSTR2/SSTR5-overexpressing lines as negative/cross-reactivity controls (SSTR family cross-reactivity is the classic failure mode — *outside knowledge*).
- **AR-null lines (PC-3, DU145) and NEPC line (NCI-H660)** as lineage controls for A1/A5.
- **Surface vs total discrimination:** permeabilised vs non-permeabilised flow; surface biotinylation as an orthogonal method for the primary endpoint.
- **Vehicle/DMSO and non-targeting siRNA**; charcoal-stripped-serum control for serum effects independent of AR.
- **IHC controls:** isotype, SSTR1-KO xenograft block, and AR/NEPC co-staining to attribute loss to AR status vs lineage switch.

---

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

H1 is falsified if **any** of the following hold:

1. Surface SSTR1 protein changes <20% after 72 h enzalutamide, androgen withdrawal **and** AR knockdown in ≥2 AR+ SSTR1+ lines, while AR-output controls (KLK3, TMPRSS2) move as expected [primary falsifier].
2. R1881 fails to increase SSTR1 mRNA/surface protein in androgen-deprived AR+ cells.
3. SSTR1 mRNA is equally expressed in AR-null lines (PC-3, DU145) as in AR+ lines.
4. In human tissue, membranous SSTR1 positivity rate in post-ARSI mCRPC is statistically indistinguishable from hormone-sensitive disease.
5. In silico: the CRPC and ARSI associations disappear under limma and after adjustment for AR activity/NEPC status, i.e. the AR link was an artifact of the simpler model.

Note: outcomes 1–4 falsify H1 but are **good news for the programme** — they mean the target is not slaved to AR. This is the design intent: the experiment is informative in both directions.

---

## 9. Uncertainty / limitations

- **Biggest uncertainty:** SSTR1 is statistically borderline in both cohorts (GSE35988 FDR 0.047; SU2C site-adjusted p 0.077, FDR 0.33) [evidence], and mRNA is not surface protein [evidence]. The entire hypothesis could rest on a real but small and partly site-driven effect.
- The direction of the lab's existing SSTR1 bench result is not given in the packet; the plan's gates assume consistency with "down in CRPC" [assumption].
- The composition panel did not score neural/neuroendocrine content [inference from the listed scores], leaving A3 untested until step 0.
- ARSI exposure ≠ resistance; cell-line enzalutamide for 72 h models acute AR blockade, not years of selection [evidence/inference]. A long-term enzalutamide-resistant derivative should be added at step 1c if available at no extra cost.
- Agilent array vs FPKM RNA-seq cross-platform comparison; low-expressed genes are underpowered in SU2C [evidence].
- Single public replication cohort; no third cohort tested.

## 10. Feedback into the next round of analysis (either outcome)

**If H1 is confirmed (surface SSTR1 is AR-dependent and falls with ARSI):**
- Re-query SU2C for the **subpopulation that retains SSTR1**: AR-high, non-NEPC, ARSI-naive, or specific metastatic sites — define an addressable population and its prevalence [analysis task].
- Re-frame SSTR1 as a **pharmacodynamic/AR-activity biomarker** rather than a target, and test whether SSTR1 + DPP4 + TRPM8 form a compact AR-output readout that outperforms single genes [analysis task].
- Explore whether AR-restoring or androgen-priming strategies transiently upregulate surface SSTR1 to create a dosing window [speculation — would need its own cheap in vitro test, already covered by the R1881 arm of step 1c].
- Apply the same AR-dependence filter to all tier-1 candidates before any of them get bench resource [inference].

**If H1 is falsified (surface SSTR1 maintained under AR blockade):**
- Formally de-weight mRNA-level inference for surface targets across the whole candidate table; add a "protein/mRNA discordance" caveat to the tiering rules [analysis task].
- Re-run the pipeline with limma, a neuroendocrine/neural composition score, and AR-activity adjustment, and re-rank surface-annotated genes by *predicted protein retention* rather than fold-change [analysis task].
- Proceed to step 2 (tissue) with higher priority, since the therapeutic case is now open.

**Either way:**
- Add the measured cell-line SSTR1 levels and KO-validated reagent performance to the data packet so future candidates are screened for reagent availability *before* tiering [process change].
- Record whether DPP4/TRPM8 behaved as predicted in the same assay — this is a direct, cheap validation of the whole public-data pipeline [inference].
