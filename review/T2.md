# SSTR1 and lower expression in treatment-resistant prostate cancer: what the public-data packet supports

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

**Answer to Q1 (closest to causality).** The SU2C replication model comes closest: `expression ~ ARSI exposure + biopsy site`, one biopsy per patient, AR as positive control.

- **What it supports:** that prior ARSI exposure is followed by lower SSTR1 mRNA in metastatic tissue. Exposure comes before the biopsy, so the direction is treatment → expression.
- **What it does not test:** whether low SSTR1 causes resistance, which is the opposite direction. ARSI exposure is also not the same as ARSI resistance.
- **The SSTR1 result itself is not significant.** Site-adjusted beta is −0.34 (about 21% lower), p = 0.077. The unadjusted p of 0.013 weakens once biopsy site is accounted for.

**Answer to Q3 (best-supported mechanism).** The material most supports **loss of AR transcriptional output under ARSI pressure** (H1 below). That support is weak and partly by analogy to other genes:

- The tier-1 genes TRPM8 and DPP4 behave this way.
- Androgen Response is the top down-regulated pathway.
- SSTR1 correlates only weakly with AR activity (rho 0.19, about 4% of rank variance).

The data do not exclude two other explanations:

- A non-tumor-intrinsic composition or purity effect (H3). The SU2C effect is concentrated in lymph node and bone biopsies, where the prostate-tissue composition check does not apply.
- An AR-independent stable silencing mechanism (H2), which was never tested.

**Recommendation.** Do not treat "SSTR1 is AR-regulated and lost in resistance" as established.

1. First run cheap reanalyses of data you already have (section 6, step 0).
2. Then run one short perturbation experiment that separates H1 from H2: ARSI/androgen deprivation with DHT rescue, then EZH2 inhibition in resistant lines.
3. Then do in situ localization (RNAscope plus a tumor-cell co-stain) to address H3.
4. If a ligand-based therapy is the goal, measure surface SSTR1 protein before anything else downstream.

## 3. Reasoning

### Q1: why the SU2C analysis is the closest to causality

None of the analyses is interventional, so all are associational. The SU2C model is the only one whose design removes several confounders that are built into the discovery comparison.

**Alternatives the SU2C design excludes, or at least weakens:**

1. **Localized vs metastatic biology.** Every sample is mCRPC.
2. **Surgical vs rapid-autopsy collection** (ischemia, post-mortem RNA changes). Every sample is a clinical biopsy.
3. **Castration status.** Every patient is castration-resistant, so the contrast isolates ARSI on top of ADT.
4. **Differences in the mix of biopsy sites** between exposed and naive groups. Site is a covariate.
5. **Pseudo-replication.** One biopsy per patient.
6. **Gene-level multiplicity for SSTR1.** It was pre-specified, so its single p-value can be read without the 300-gene FDR. Even read that way, the site-adjusted p is 0.077.
7. **Assay insensitivity.** The AR positive control shows the model can detect real ARSI-associated changes.

**Alternatives that remain open:**

1. **Confounding by indication or treatment history.** ARSI-exposed patients are likely later-line, with longer disease duration and possibly more taxane exposure. None of these is modelled.
2. **Within-site tumor purity and microenvironment.** Site adjustment removes between-site differences, not purity differences within a site. The prostate-derived composition scores do not cover bone marrow or lymph node cells.
3. **Selection or survivorship.** Tumors that persist and get biopsied after ARSI may be selected for a low-SSTR1 state rather than having had SSTR1 switched off. This is clonal selection versus regulation.
4. **Exposure is not resistance**, as the material notes.
5. **Mechanism.** The design cannot say whether the drop is AR-mediated, epigenetic, genomic or compositional.
6. **Statistical fragility.** The attenuation from p 0.013 to 0.077 after site adjustment suggests that biopsy-site composition explains part of the raw signal.

The GSE35988 composition check (analysis step 2) also excludes an alternative, namely stromal or immune contamination. But it does so within a comparison that is otherwise confounded by stage, site, treatment and collection method, so it does less to move toward causality.

### Q3: why H1 is favored, and only weakly

- **For H1:**
  - It is the parsimonious explanation given the strong AR-output signal in tier-1 genes.
  - SSTR1 correlates positively with AR activity.
  - Composition shows no correlation in localized tumors.
- **Against a strong H1:**
  - The SSTR1 effect (−0.34) is about a third of the size seen for TRPM8 and DPP4.
  - The AR correlation is weak.
  - The effect weakens with site adjustment.
  - The packet contains no direct evidence of AR binding or androgen responsiveness for SSTR1.
  - A gene co-varying with AR output across tumors may instead be co-varying with luminal differentiation or tumor purity.
- **H2 is not contradicted, but nothing in the packet tests it.**
- **H3 is argued against by the prostate composition check** but supported somewhat by the attenuation from site adjustment and the concentration in lymph node and bone.

## 4. Assumptions

- **Gene identity.** The "candidate gene" is SSTR1, the pre-specified gene with the bench result. The six tier-1 genes are also lower after ARSI, but the question's framing fits SSTR1.
- **Platform comparability.** Agilent array log ratios and RNA-seq FPKM measure the same transcript reasonably well. A low FPKM floor may compress the true difference.
- **AR activity score.** It reflects tumor-cell AR output, not luminal or benign content. The packet does not define the score.
- **Composition scores.** Marker-based scores capture the main contaminants in localized tumors. They do not transfer to bone or lymph node metastases.
- **Exposure as proxy.** ARSI exposure in SU2C is treated as a proxy for treatment pressure, not resistance.
- **mRNA vs protein.** mRNA changes are assumed to have some relationship to surface protein. This is unverified; the packet flags it.

## 5. Alternative explanations

Three mechanistically distinct hypotheses (Q2) follow, treated as equals.

### H1: Reversible loss of AR-driven transcription

**Mechanism.** SSTR1 is a direct or indirect AR target. ARSI suppresses AR output, so SSTR1 falls within tumor cells. This is the authors' preferred explanation.

**Predicted observation.**
- SSTR1 tracks canonical AR targets (KLK3, TMPRSS2, FKBP5, TRPM8) within the ARSI-exposed and naive groups separately.
- In SU2C, adding AR-activity score to the model should largely absorb the ARSI coefficient (mediation).
- In AR+ lines, SSTR1 falls within 24–72 h of enzalutamide or charcoal-stripped serum and recovers with DHT.

**Falsifying experiment.** Time course in LNCaP, VCaP and LAPC4 (outside knowledge: these are standard AR+ lines; check that SSTR1 is detectable at baseline first):
- vehicle
- enzalutamide 10 µM
- charcoal-stripped serum (CSS)
- CSS plus DHT 1–10 nM
- AR siRNA

Read out by RT-qPCR at 24/48/72 h. Optionally add AR ChIP-qPCR, or reuse public AR ChIP-seq, at the SSTR1 locus.

**Result that argues against H1.** Canonical targets drop at least 4-fold while SSTR1 stays within about 1.3-fold of vehicle, or does not recover with DHT, or does not fall with AR knockdown. In SU2C, the ARSI beta remaining essentially unchanged after adjusting for AR score would also argue against it.

### H2: Stable, AR-independent epigenetic silencing selected or induced in resistant cells

**Mechanism.** Silencing via PRC2/EZH2 (H3K27me3) or promoter DNA methylation, arising with lineage reprogramming or clonal selection under therapy. EZH2 rises in CRPC in the material.

**Predicted observation.**
- SSTR1 low correlates with high EZH2 or PRC2 target signatures, or with SSTR1 promoter methylation, more strongly than with AR score.
- Low SSTR1 persists when androgen is restored.
- Resistant derivative lines keep low SSTR1 even with AR active.

**Falsifying experiment.** In ARSI-resistant derivative lines (outside knowledge: e.g. enzalutamide-resistant LNCaP derivatives, or long-term CSS-adapted lines):
- Measure H3K27me3 (CUT&RUN or ChIP-qPCR) and CpG methylation at the SSTR1 promoter.
- Treat with an EZH2 inhibitor (e.g. tazemetostat) or 5-aza-2′-deoxycytidine, with or without DHT.

**Result that argues against H2.** No enrichment of H3K27me3 or methylation at SSTR1 relative to parental cells. SSTR1 not restored by EZH2 inhibition or demethylation despite verified loss of global H3K27me3. Or SSTR1 fully restored by DHT alone in the resistant line, which would point back to H1.

### H3: Not a tumor-cell change, but composition, purity or site

**Mechanism.** SSTR1 is expressed partly in non-malignant cells (e.g. vascular, neural or specific stromal elements not captured by the marker scores), or tumor purity differs between ARSI-exposed and naive biopsies within bone and lymph node. The bulk signal then shifts without any change per tumor cell.

**Predicted observation.**
- In tissue, SSTR1 signal sits largely outside tumor epithelium.
- In SU2C, adding a tumor-purity estimate removes the ARSI effect.
- The effect is confined to low-purity sites. The packet already notes concentration in lymph node and bone.

**Falsifying experiment.**
- RNAscope for SSTR1 with co-stain for tumor cells (AR or PanCK/AMACR), on ARSI-naive and exposed metastatic biopsies, ideally matched pre/post pairs, with per-cell quantification in tumor cells only.
- In silico, re-fit the SU2C model with a purity covariate. Outside knowledge: SU2C publications provide purity estimates; alternatively compute an ESTIMATE-type score.

**Result that argues against H3.** SSTR1 transcripts localize predominantly to tumor cells and are lower per tumor cell after ARSI, and the SU2C beta is stable after purity adjustment.

### Other explanations not chosen as the main three

- **Genomic loss.** Copy-number deletion at the SSTR1 locus. Checkable directly in SU2C copy-number data on cBioPortal.
- **Statistical artefact.** The discovery result is borderline on both thresholds; the replication is not significant after site adjustment.
- **Confounding by treatment history.** This is a design confounder rather than a mechanism, but it could produce the association whichever biological mechanism is true.

- It is not clearly distinguishable from clonal silencing (B).
- Confidence in that ranking is low (see section 10).

**Recommendation:** run discriminating bench experiments before committing to a ligand-based SSTR1 strategy. Test AR dependence, heritability of the loss, and cell-type origin, and measure surface protein.

# 2. Evidence

**Direct evidence**
- SSTR1 is lower in CRPC than in localized tumors (log2FC −1.18, FDR 0.047).
  - The log2FC barely clears the |log2FC| > 1 threshold, and the FDR is borderline.
  - The test was Welch's t-test, not limma, and the authors say this could move borderline genes like SSTR1.
- Composition rho = 0.0146 (max across stroma, immune, mast-cell and early-response scores), so SSTR1 is not flagged as a composition or handling artifact.
- In SU2C, SSTR1 is lower after ARSI exposure (unadjusted p = 0.013; site-adjusted beta −0.34, p = 0.077; FDR 0.334). The effect is concentrated in lymph node and bone biopsies.
- SSTR1 correlates with AR activity (rho 0.19, p = 0.002). This is statistically detectable but explains only about 4% of variance.

**Indirect evidence**
- The retained down-in-CRPC genes are enriched for Androgen Response (41/100, adj p = 1.08e-11).
- The six replicating genes are lower after ARSI, and TRPM8 and DPP4 are androgen-regulated.
- The AR positive control behaves as expected (higher after ARSI, p = 3e-5). This validates the pipeline's ability to detect an ARSI contrast, not SSTR1 specifically.
- EZH2 is up in CRPC. This is a loose hint relevant to Hypothesis B.
- The authors mention a bench result consistent with SSTR1 loss, but no bench data are in the material, so I cannot weigh it.

# 3. Reasoning

**What the SU2C design excludes (for SSTR1)**
- **Autopsy versus surgical collection:** SU2C compares metastatic biopsies with each other, so this discovery-cohort confound is removed.
- **Primary versus metastasis:** this is also removed, because all samples are metastatic.
- **Biopsy-site differences:** partly handled by adjustment. Note that the adjustment moved p from 0.013 to 0.077, so site explains some of the signal.
- **Pseudoreplication:** excluded by using one biopsy per patient.
- **Gross stromal, immune and stress artifacts:** partly excluded in discovery only. The scores were tested across localized tumors, not across SU2C metastases.

**What remains open**
- **Confounding by indication or disease stage:** ARSI-exposed patients are likely later-line, with other prior therapy and more advanced disease.
- **Exposure is not resistance:** ARSI exposure does not equal ARSI resistance, as the authors note.
- **Adaptive versus selective change:** there are no paired pre/post samples, so adaptive regulation cannot be separated from clonal selection.
- **Tumor purity:** this is not modeled in SU2C, and marker scores are not a full deconvolution.
- **Power:** FPKM data give limited power for low-expressed genes. SSTR1's median log2FPKM is 1.25.
- **Direction of effect:** a gene that is low and also AR-correlated cannot show whether AR loss causes SSTR1 loss.
- **AR mRNA paradox:** AR is higher in CRPC while AR output is lower. "Loss of AR output" is therefore not simply AR mRNA loss.

# 4. Assumptions

- The SU2C RNA-seq expression is comparable in kind to the discovery Agilent array data.
- Marker-based scores capture the main non-tumor contributions.
- ARSI exposure is a reasonable proxy for ARSI-driven selective or adaptive pressure.
- The SSTR1 mRNA difference reflects tumor-cell expression. This is unverified.

# 5. Alternative explanations

Three mechanistically distinct explanations for the lower expression. The authors' implied preference, SSTR1 downstream of AR, is **A**, treated here as one hypothesis among three.

**A. AR-dependent transcriptional regulation (adaptive, tumor-cell-intrinsic)**
- **Predicts:**
  - SSTR1 falls when AR output is suppressed by ARSI, ADT or AR knockdown.
  - It recovers with androgen add-back.
  - It tracks direct AR-target scores across tumors and within patients over time.
  - AR binds at or near the locus (outside knowledge: AR ChIP-seq data would test this; not in the material).
- **Falsifying experiment:**
  - Treat AR+ lines (e.g., LNCaP, VCaP) with enzalutamide or AR siRNA, with or without R1881 or DHT rescue.
  - Run an RNA/qPCR time course and AR ChIP.
- **Result against:**
  - SSTR1 is unchanged on AR loss.
  - SSTR1 does not recover with androgen.
  - No AR occupancy is found.
  - SSTR1 stays low in tumors with preserved AR output.
- **Weakness in the packet:** rho 0.19 is modest.

**B. Clonal selection with stable genetic or epigenetic silencing**
- **Predicts:**
  - The SSTR1-low state is heritable and not reversed by androgen.
  - It is associated with locus copy-number loss, promoter methylation or H3K27me3.
  - It is subclonal or heterogeneous in tumors.
  - It is present in ARSI-resistant derived lines.
  - EZH2 is a candidate regulator, since EZH2 is up in CRPC (material). Methylation is outside knowledge.
- **Falsifying experiment:**
  - Compare parental and ARSI-resistant derivative lines or PDX/organoids.
  - Run bisulfite-seq and H3K27me3 ChIP.
  - Check copy number (outside knowledge: SU2C also has DNA data that could be queried).
  - Test whether EZH2 or DNMT inhibition restores expression.
  - Use lineage barcoding to distinguish pre-existing SSTR1-low clones from induced loss.
- **Result against:**
  - No copy-number, methylation or chromatin differences.
  - EZH2 and DNMT inhibition do not restore expression.
  - SSTR1 recovers with androgen add-back (this would favor A).
  - Barcoded clones all decline uniformly (this would favor induction over selection).

**C. Non-tumor-cell or sampling-context effect (composition, purity, site)**
- **Predicts:**
  - SSTR1 is expressed in a diluted cell compartment, such as benign luminal epithelium or another non-tumor cell.
  - Lower bulk expression tracks tumor purity or site (the lymph node and bone concentration is suggestive).
  - The effect vanishes after purity adjustment.
- **Falsifying experiment:**
  - Add purity covariates in SU2C (outside knowledge: purity estimates exist from DNA data).
  - Use scRNA-seq or deconvolution, RNAscope or IHC with epithelial segmentation, or laser-capture microdissection of tumor cells.
- **Result against:**
  - SSTR1 remains lower in purified tumor epithelium.
  - The effect persists after purity and site adjustment.
  - Cell-type localization shows the tumor cell is the main source.
- **Packet evidence:** rho 0.0146 argues against C, but this was tested only in localized tumors with marker scores.

A and B are not mutually exclusive. Early adaptive loss can become fixed epigenetically.

# 6. Proposed experiment / next step

1. **Reanalysis (cheap, run first):**
   - Rerun SSTR1 with limma.
   - Add tumor purity and treatment-line covariates in SU2C.
   - Stratify by site.
   - Test AR-score-adjusted models and ask whether ARSI still predicts SSTR1 after adjusting for AR activity.
   - This asks whether SSTR1 loss is explained by AR output (A) or exceeds it (B or C).
2. **Cell-line AR manipulation (tests A):** ARSI and AR knockdown with androgen rescue, then washout.
3. **Resistant derivatives (tests B):** compare parental and ARSI-resistant lines, plus epigenetic-inhibitor rescue.
4. **In-situ and cell-type resolution (tests C):** RNAscope or IHC on matched tissue, plus microdissection.
5. **Protein and surface-level validation:** measure SSTR1 receptor protein at the cell surface, since mRNA is not protein and surface protein is what matters for ligand-based therapy.
6. **Paired longitudinal patient samples (if accessible):** pre- and post-ARSI biopsies from the same patient.

# 7. Key controls

- **Positive controls:** AR itself, plus KLK3, TMPRSS2, TRPM8 and DPP4 as androgen-responsive readouts (KLK3 and TMPRSS2 are outside knowledge).
- **Negative controls:** non-AR-regulated genes that should not change.
- **Rescue and washout:** androgen add-back and drug washout, to separate reversible from fixed changes.
- **Multiple lines:** use both AR-dependent and AR-independent lines, with matched passage.
- **Purity and composition:** purity-matched samples and composition scores in SU2C.
- **Assay controls:** antibody validation with SSTR1 knockdown for protein assays, and appropriate normalization.

# 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

For the best-supported hypothesis, A:
- no AR dependence in vitro (SSTR1 unchanged on AR knockdown or ARSI, and not restored by androgen);
- no AR binding at the locus;
- after adjusting for AR activity, the ARSI effect on SSTR1 is unchanged, with AR activity explaining little;
- paired samples show SSTR1 falling while AR output is maintained.

If epigenetic inhibitors restore expression independently of androgen, B is favored over A. If the effect disappears after purity adjustment or microdissection, C is favored over both.

# 9. Uncertainty / limitations

- SSTR1's evidence is borderline at each step: FDR 0.047 in discovery, adjusted p = 0.077 and FDR 0.334 in replication.
- CRPC versus localized mixes castration, metastasis, chemotherapy and autopsy versus surgery. The composition filter does not remove all of these, and marker scores are not deconvolution.
- Post-mortem RNA effects on low-expressed genes are not fully addressed.
- The AR correlation is weak (rho 0.19).
- There are no paired pre/post data, no genomic or epigenomic data and no protein data. A and B cannot be separated with the packet.
- The packet is a single small run, and its stated purpose is to find failure modes, not to establish effects.
- The bench result the authors mention is not in the material.
- The comparison is exposure, not resistance, and mRNA is not protein.

# 10. Confidence: 30%

This is my confidence that Hypothesis A is the best-supported explanation and is at least a partial contributor. Confidence is low because the SSTR1 evidence is borderline and nothing in the packet discriminates A from B.
