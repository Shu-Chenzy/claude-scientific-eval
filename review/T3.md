## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

The single most important structural fact in this packet is that **11 of the 13 candidates are *down* in CRPC**, while the available toolkit (CRISPR KO, CRISPRi) only removes expression. That is a legitimate and interesting question, but it is a different experiment, and it must be run in an AR-active, ARSI-sensitive model, not in a CRPC model. Only one candidate in the table (**TONSL**) is up in CRPC and therefore direction-matched to a conventional dependency screen.

## 3. Reasoning

### Why DPP4 first

1. **It is the only candidate where every tier of evidence is simultaneously strong**: largest discovery effect, best replication FDR, clean composition score, highest expression (so the SU2C estimate is not a low-count artifact), and cell-surface localization.
2. **It is the only candidate where I can phenocopy the genetic manipulation pharmacologically in week 1.** Gliptins (outside knowledge) let me run a drug arm in parallel with CRISPRi, so a null CRISPRi result is interpretable rather than a knockdown-efficiency excuse — and concordance between catalytic inhibition and knockdown distinguishes enzymatic from scaffolding function.
3. **The direction-matched question is clinically meaningful**: if DPP4 loss is *permissive* for AR-independent or ARSI-tolerant growth, that is both a resistance mechanism and a safety signal for widely prescribed diabetes drugs. If DPP4 loss is merely downstream of AR shutdown, that is a clean negative that retires the whole "AR-output-reporter" tier at once.
4. **Live-cell imaging is the right readout**: ARSI-tolerant outgrowth is a slow-growth/persister phenotype best captured by continuous confluence tracking over 2–3 weeks, not an endpoint viability assay.

**Major risk for DPP4:** it is explicitly named in the packet as androgen-regulated. The most likely outcome is that DPP4 is a faithful *readout* of AR activity with no causal role, so CRISPRi produces no proliferation or drug-tolerance phenotype. Mitigation: the drug arm costs almost nothing and the AR-activity readout (KLK3/TMPRSS2) turns a null into an informative epistasis result.

### Why TONSL second

1. **Direction-matching dominates under a four-week constraint.** With CRISPR KO/CRISPRi as the core tool, an up-regulated gene gives a loss-of-function experiment whose interpretation is immediate (dependency yes/no) rather than conditional on a resistance-induction protocol.
2. **It is the molecular handle on the packet's strongest surviving biology.** The up-hit signal is overwhelmingly E2F/G2-M/mitotic — i.e. proliferation. Rather than test a generic proliferation marker, TONSL offers a mechanistic hypothesis (replication-fork/HR dependency, outside knowledge) with a drug-combination readout available within the same four weeks.
3. **Live-cell imaging is unusually informative here**: replication-stress and mitotic phenotypes (prolonged mitosis, micronuclei, mitotic catastrophe) are visible by time-lapse within 72 h, so I get a phenotype before the organoid arm finishes.
4. **It hedges the DPP4 risk.** DPP4 and TONSL are non-redundant: one tests "does loss of AR output *cause* anything," the other tests "is the proliferative program *dependent* on anything." If DPP4 is a passenger, the four weeks are not wasted.

**Major risk for TONSL:** it is tier 2 — it failed SU2C replication at FDR 0.14 *and* its SU2C beta points the wrong way. Two readings are possible: (i) the discovery signal is a proliferation-index artifact of comparing autopsy metastases to prostatectomy specimens; (ii) TONSL tracks proliferation, which is not what ARSI exposure per se modulates, so SU2C was the wrong replication test for it. There is also a real chance TONSL is broadly essential across lineages, making it a non-selective target.

### How my ranking changes under alternative objectives

**(a) Largest biological effect**
1. **TONSL** — a genuine fork-protection/HR dependency produces a catastrophic, unambiguous phenotype (proliferation arrest, mitotic failure, synthetic lethality with ATRi/PARPi), far larger than anything a modulatory surface protein will give. Promote to #1.
2. **TRPM8** — largest measured magnitude in both cohorts (log2FC −5.19; beta −1.15) and, as an ion channel, capable of large acute phenotypes (Ca²⁺ flux, migration, viability) under agonist challenge.
3. DPP4 drops to #3: strong statistics, but surface proteases usually give modest, context-dependent growth effects.
4. ST6GALNAC1 enters consideration (glycan rewiring can be a large phenotype) but is unmeasurable with the listed tools.

**(b) Fastest experimental validation**
1. **TRPM8** — promote to #1. A Ca²⁺ dye plus icilin/menthol on existing AR-positive lines gives a functional, quantitative, same-week answer on whether the protein is present and functional, with no CRISPR lead time. This is the single fastest decisive datapoint in the whole table.
2. **DPP4** — near-tied: commercial fluorogenic activity assay plus gliptins gives a protein-level answer in days and closes the packet's own "mRNA is not protein" gap without any genetic engineering.
3. TONSL falls to #3: CRISPRi line construction, selection and a proliferation time-course realistically consume most of the four weeks.
4. SSTR1 remains last on speed because the quantity that matters (surface receptor protein) requires a validated antibody or labelled ligand that is not in the listed resource set.

## 5. Alternative explanations

1. **The entire tier-1 list is an AR-activity thermometer.** Down-hits are enriched for Androgen Response (padj 1e-11); DPP4 and TRPM8 are named androgen-regulated; SSTR1 correlates with AR activity. The simplest explanation for all six replicated hits is that ARSI shuts off AR output and these genes are output. Under this model, none of them is causal and all CRISPRi arms will be null.
2. **Lineage shift, not gene-specific regulation.** ARSI-exposed tumors are enriched for AR-low/NEPC phenotypes (outside knowledge). Loss of DPP4/TRPM8/SSTR1 may simply mark the fraction of samples that have changed lineage. The SU2C model adjusts for biopsy site but, as presented, not for lineage or AR-activity score.
3. **Proliferation-index confounding for TONSL.** Rapid-autopsy metastases are intrinsically more proliferative than prostatectomy specimens; the entire E2F/G2-M signature — and TONSL with it — could be a property of the comparison rather than of castration resistance. The discordant SU2C beta is consistent with this.
4. **Platform and handling artifacts not captured by marker scores.** Agilent arrays vs RNA-seq, autopsy RNA degradation, and the packet's own caveat that marker-based scores are "not a full deconvolution" mean some residual confounding survives, especially for the rho 0.4–0.43 genes.
5. **Statistical fragility.** Welch's t-test rather than limma; SSTR1 is explicitly flagged as the kind of borderline gene that could move. Candidate tiering is partly an artifact of threshold placement.
6. **Reverse causality.** DPP4 loss could be a consequence of the resistant state (epigenetic silencing accompanying AR program shutdown) rather than a cause, which would still make it a biomarker but not a target.

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

### Arm A — DPP4: does loss of DPP4 enable ARSI tolerance?

**First experiment (start day 1):** CRISPRi knockdown of DPP4 (2 independent sgRNAs + non-targeting control) in an AR-positive, enzalutamide-sensitive line, then a 14-day enzalutamide dose–response (0, 1, 5, 10 µM) with continuous live-cell confluence imaging. Run in parallel a drug-only phenocopy arm: vehicle vs gliptin (dose range) ± enzalutamide in the parental line, with no genetic manipulation, so results exist by day 10 regardless of CRISPRi timing.

- **Primary readout:** area-under-the-growth-curve and time-to-regrowth under enzalutamide, knockdown vs control.
- **Secondary:** AR-output qPCR (KLK3, TMPRSS2, NKX3-1) ±knockdown, to test whether DPP4 sits upstream of, downstream of, or parallel to AR output.
- **Week 3–4:** if a ≥1.5-fold AUC difference is seen, repeat in one AR-driven organoid (CRISPRi if transducible, otherwise gliptin ± enzalutamide only) with imaging-based organoid area tracking.
- **Decision rule:** no reproducible enzalutamide-tolerance phenotype across both sgRNAs and the drug arm ⇒ DPP4 is an AR-output passenger; retire it and the androgen-regulated tier with it.

### Arm B — TONSL: is it a selective dependency in the proliferative CRPC program?

**First experiment (start day 1):** CRISPR KO and CRISPRi of TONSL (2 sgRNAs each + non-targeting) in a CRPC-like, high-proliferation line and a matched AR-driven/benign-ish comparator, with 7-day live-cell growth curves plus 24–48 h time-lapse mitotic phenotyping (mitotic duration, mitotic catastrophe, micronucleus frequency).

- **Primary readout:** differential growth inhibition between CRPC-like and comparator line (selectivity index), not absolute inhibition.
- **Secondary:** ATR-inhibitor and PARP-inhibitor dose–response in knockdown vs control (synergy would both confirm the mechanistic hypothesis and supply a drug strategy).
- **Decision rule:** equal inhibition in both lines ⇒ pan-essential, not a selective target ⇒ deprioritize to a background project. CRPC-selective inhibition plus a mitotic/replication-stress phenotype ⇒ escalate to organoids in month 2.

### Zero-cost add-on (strongly recommended)

Run a 2-day TRPM8 Ca²⁺-imaging check (icilin/menthol challenge) on the same AR-positive line, ± enzalutamide pretreatment. It consumes almost no time, directly tests whether functional protein tracks the mRNA signal, and determines whether TRPM8 deserves the next four weeks.

## 7. Key controls

**Genetic**
- ≥2 independent sgRNAs per gene; non-targeting and a safe-harbour/olfactory-receptor control sgRNA.
- Knockdown/knockout verification at **protein** level (the packet's own caveat: mRNA is not protein) — Western or surface flow for DPP4, Western for TONSL.
- Rescue: sgRNA-resistant cDNA re-expression for any positive hit; catalytically dead DPP4 mutant to separate enzymatic from scaffold function.
- A positive-control sgRNA with known essentiality (e.g. an AR sgRNA in an AR-dependent line) to confirm the CRISPRi system is functional in each model.

**Pharmacological**
- Vehicle (DMSO) matched by volume; full dose–response rather than single dose; a second structurally distinct gliptin to control for off-target chemistry; enzalutamide dose–response in parental cells to establish the sensitivity window.
- Concordance test: does the drug phenocopy the genetic knockdown? Divergence indicates non-catalytic function or off-target drug effects.

**Imaging/assay**
- Seeding-density matching and confluence-normalised growth metrics; drug-free wells for plate-edge/evaporation effects.
- Blinded or automated scoring of mitotic phenotypes.

**Biological/system**
- AR-pathway readout (KLK3) in every condition, so I always know whether I have perturbed AR output as a confounder.
- STR authentication and mycoplasma testing of all lines and organoids.
- Document baseline DPP4/TONSL expression in every model before interpreting any null result.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

**DPP4 hypothesis** ("DPP4 loss contributes causally to ARSI tolerance") is falsified if:
- Two independent sgRNAs achieving >70% protein knockdown produce no change in enzalutamide-tolerant outgrowth (AUC within ~20% of control) in both 2D and organoid formats; **and**
- Pharmacological DPP4 inhibition likewise produces no change; **and**
- Knockdown does not alter AR-output genes.
Additionally falsifying: if DPP4 protein turns out to be undetectable in all available AR-positive models, the tissue-level signal cannot be modelled with these tools and the hypothesis is untestable here (a different kind of stop).

**TONSL hypothesis** ("TONSL is a selective dependency of the CRPC proliferative program") is falsified if:
- Knockout/knockdown inhibits growth equally in CRPC-like and non-CRPC comparator lines (selectivity index ≈ 1) — i.e. pan-essential, not CRPC-specific; **or**
- Knockdown produces no growth or mitotic phenotype at all despite verified protein loss; **or**
- No interaction with ATR/PARP inhibition is detectable, removing the mechanistic rationale and the therapeutic handle.

**Falsifying the broader framing:** if CRISPRi of DPP4, TRPM8 and one further AR-regulated candidate all produce null phenotypes while AR knockdown works in the same experiment, the parsimonious conclusion is that the replicated tier-1 signature is an AR-activity readout with no causal content, and the project should pivot from the down-going candidates to the up-going E2F/G2-M program.
