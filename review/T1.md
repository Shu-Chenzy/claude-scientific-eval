## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

Three conclusions are best supported by the packet:

- **C1 (artifact filter):** The raw CRPC-vs-localized hit list is dominated by composition and handling signals. After removing composition-flagged genes, the remaining hits show a coherent tumor-associated pattern.
- **C2 (AR-linked replication):** Six genes (DPP4, ST6GALNAC1, TRPM8, SNTG2, TSPAN11, AGBL2) are lower in CRPC and lower in ARSI-exposed mCRPC in SU2C. This is consistent with reduced AR-linked output under ARSI pressure.
- **C3 (retained CRPC program):** Among retained hits, proliferation gene sets (E2F targets, G2-M checkpoint, mitotic spindle) are up in CRPC, and Androgen Response is down.

SSTR1 is not among the three strongest conclusions. Its evidence is borderline (site-adjusted FDR 0.334; nominal p = 0.077), and it is treated as a hypothesis in Section 9.

**Recommendation:** Before committing bench resources, run cheap in-silico checks (purity, AR-activity and treatment-history adjustment; limma; a preset replication threshold). Then test whether the six C2 genes respond to androgen and AR inhibition in AR-positive models, and measure SSTR1 protein at the cell surface.

## 2. Evidence

### Direct evidence (what the data show)

**C1**
- 1,282 of 5,746 hits (34% of downregulated, 3% of upregulated) have a composition or handling correlation with rho ≥ 0.5 across localized tumors. These include 20 of the top 25 hits.
- The top composition-flagged genes (e.g., PAGE4, FCER1A, MS4A2, ACTG2) have very small FDRs and high composition rho values (0.57 to 0.89).
- Retained upregulated hits are enriched for E2F targets (adj. P 3.38e-20) and G2-M checkpoint (adj. P 1.73e-16). Retained downregulated hits are enriched for Androgen Response (adj. P 1.08e-11).

**C2**
- All six genes are lower in CRPC (FDR < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1 in GSE35988; DPP4 log2FC -4.78, TRPM8 -5.19, ST6GALNAC1 -4.18, SNTG2 -2.76, TSPAN11 -2.71, AGBL2 -2.73).
- All six have negative site-adjusted betas in SU2C ARSI-exposed vs ARSI-naive (FDR < 0.10; DPP4 FDR 0.0026, TRPM8 0.0041, ST6GALNAC1 0.0030, SNTG2 0.031, TSPAN11 0.046, AGBL2 0.100).
- The AR positive control rises after ARSI (p = 3e-5), so the pipeline detects the expected direction for at least one known gene.

**C3**
- Hallmark enrichment on retained hits (listed above). AR, EZH2 and AURKA are described as rising in CRPC, but no values for these genes appear in the packet.

### Indirect evidence (interpretation added on top of the data)

**C1:** The flagged genes are interpreted as "not tumor biology." The data show only a correlation with stroma, immune, mast-cell or early-response scores within localized tumors. The retained set is interpreted as "coherent tumor biology," which assumes the filter removed the confounders.

**C2:** The six genes are interpreted as reflecting "loss of AR output under ARSI pressure." This relies on the packet's statement that DPP4 and TRPM8 are androgen-regulated (this is an external claim, not shown in the packet; see Section 9). It also assumes ARSI exposure is a reasonable proxy for AR pressure.

**C3:** The enrichment is interpreted as CRPC proliferation and loss of androgen signaling. Over-representation on a filtered gene list is weaker evidence than a full ranked-list analysis.

## 3. Reasoning

1. Raw CRPC-vs-localized hits are contaminated by sample composition and collection handling (the packet's own analysis shows this).
2. Removing composition-flagged genes changes the enrichment toward cell-cycle and androgen-response programs, which is more plausible as tumor biology (C1, C3).
3. The subset of retained, tumor-associated hits that also appear in the independent SU2C ARSI comparison is small: six genes (C2).
4. The direction of change is consistent across discovery and replication, and the androgen-linked interpretation is offered for the most consistent signal.
5. SSTR1 has a plausible biological link to AR, but its replication is nominal and therefore not used as a top conclusion.

## 4. Assumptions

- The composition score (stroma, immune, mast cell, immediate-early genes) captures the relevant confounders. The packet notes that marker scores are not full deconvolution.
- A Spearman rho threshold of 0.5 is an adequate flag. Genes below it (e.g., TSPAN11 at 0.416, AGBL2 at 0.267) are assumed to be free of confounding.
- Direction consistency across Agilent arrays (GSE35988) and RNA-seq FPKM (SU2C) is informative despite platform differences.
- ARSI exposure in SU2C is a usable contrast for AR pressure. The packet notes it is not the same as resistance.
- Site adjustment (plus one biopsy per patient) is sufficient to handle biopsy-site confounding.
- Welch's t-test gives adequate results (the packet notes limma could change borderline genes).

## 5. Alternative explanations

- **Residual composition or purity:** Tumor cellularity may differ between localized, metastatic and ARSI-exposed samples. Tumor purity is not in the model, and proliferation genes could partly reflect higher tumor content in metastases.
- **Shared advanced-disease signature:** The six genes may be lower in any more advanced or heavily treated tumor, not specifically in response to AR pressure. The model does not adjust for time on therapy, prior chemotherapy, or disease burden.
- **Treatment and handling confounders:** CRPC vs localized mixes castration, metastasis, chemotherapy and autopsy vs surgical collection (packet limitation). Post-mortem interval is not reported.
- **Low-expression artifacts:** SNTG2 (median log2FPKM 0.095) and AGBL2 (0.188) are low-expressed. In FPKM data, these values may be dominated by noise.
- **Cross-platform mismatch:** Array and RNA-seq measurements may not be directly comparable, so concordant direction may reflect shared biology or chance.
- **Selection bias in replication:** The six were chosen from the top 300 discovery hits. The replication threshold (FDR < 0.10) is looser than the discovery threshold (FDR < 0.05).
- **Proliferation signature as sampling effect:** E2F/G2-M enrichment could reflect autopsy metastases having different cell-cycle content from prostatectomy specimens, not a CRPC-intrinsic program.

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Step 1 (in silico, low cost):**
- Re-run the CRPC-vs-localized and ARSI-vs-naive models with limma.
- Add tumor purity, a proliferation score, an AR-activity score (defined without the candidate genes), time on therapy, prior chemotherapy and site as covariates.
- Check whether the six C2 genes and SSTR1 survive.
- Report site-stratified estimates with confidence intervals and an interaction test.

**Step 2 (bench, AR-dependence of the six C2 genes):**
- In AR-positive prostate cancer cell models (outside knowledge: common examples include LNCaP-type lines), treat with vehicle vs DHT and vs enzalutamide.
- Perform AR knockdown.
- Measure the six genes by qPCR. An AR-dependent pattern should show induction by DHT and reduction by enzalutamide or AR knockdown.

**Step 3 (SSTR1 protein):**
- Perform SSTR1 immunohistochemistry on matched localized, CRPC and ARSI-exposed tissues, scored on tumor cells by a pathologist.
- Confirm cell-surface localization in a relevant cell model.

## 7. Key controls

- **Negative-control gene sets:** Composition-matched random gene sets and expression-matched non-AR-regulated tumor genes.
- **Purity and proliferation covariates:** Included in the model and used as stratifiers.
- **Positive controls:** AR target genes with known androgen responsiveness (outside knowledge: KLK3 and TMPRSS2 are commonly used; verify in your model).
- **Vehicle and DHT arms** in bench experiments, with matched cell-line authentication (outside knowledge: standard practice).
- **Antibody controls** (isotype and knockdown or blocking peptide) for SSTR1 IHC.
- **Pre-specified thresholds** for replication, set before inspecting results, as the packet did for discovery.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

- **C2 (AR-linked six-gene set):**
  - The six genes do not change with DHT vs vehicle or enzalutamide vs vehicle in AR-positive models, or do not change with AR knockdown.
  - The ARSI association disappears after adjusting for purity, AR-activity score, treatment history and site.
  - The genes are not lower in a third, independent cohort using pre-specified thresholds.
- **C1 (artifact filter):**
  - Composition-flagged genes remain differential within tumor-enriched or purity-matched samples. This would mean many flagged genes do reflect tumor biology.
- **C3 (proliferation program):**
  - The E2F and G2-M enrichment disappears after purity or proliferation adjustment, or in microdissected tumor-only data.

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

**Recommendation.** Use DPP4, TRPM8 and ST6GALNAC1 as the most defensible ARSI-associated readouts. Do not treat SSTR1 as replicated. Before any bench work, run inexpensive reanalyses of the public data (Section 6). The two most informative ones are:

Note also that every candidate is *lower* in CRPC and ARSI-exposed tumors. For a surface-directed or ligand-based therapy, the material's own Limitations section points to surface protein as the relevant quantity. A target that is lost under ARSI therefore works against that strategy.

### Conclusion A: Many top CRPC-vs-localized hits track non-tumor composition or handling signals; p-value ranking alone would prioritize them

- **Directly demonstrated:**
  - 1,282 of 5,746 hits are flagged (34% of downregulated, 3% of upregulated).

## 3. Reasoning

- **Directional concordance is the strongest feature.** All six tier-1 genes are down in both contrasts. Tiering was FDR-based and did not require concordance. Tier 2 includes discordant genes (CCPG1 and SLC35A3 are down in CRPC but higher with ARSI; TONSL is the reverse). So tier-1 concordance was not forced by the rule, which modestly strengthens it.
- **Two different, imperfect contrasts agree.** Agreement across them is more informative than either alone. It still cannot separate "ARSI lowers these genes in tumor cells" from "ARSI-exposed cohorts contain more AR-low tumors."
- **Rising AR mRNA alongside falling AR targets fits two models.** It is compatible with uniform loss of AR output, and also with a mixture of AR-high and AR-low tumors. The packet does not show an AR-activity comparison between exposed and naive patients, which would discriminate partly between them.
- **The candidates' direction cuts against surface targeting.** UniProt marks DPP4, TRPM8, AQP11 and SSTR1 as cell surface. Because all are lower in the disease states of interest, the "candidate_targets" framing is in tension with the data for any surface-directed approach.

## 4. Assumptions

- Marker scores capture composition adequately, and within-localized correlation is a valid proxy for between-group composition artifacts.
- The 27 CRPC samples are independent. The number of patients is not stated; *outside knowledge:* rapid-autopsy series can contribute several metastases per patient. Welch's t-test assumes independence.
- ARSI exposure annotation in SU2C is accurate, and biopsy site is the main confounder.
- FPKM is adequate for low-expressed genes.
- "log2FPKM" is a consistent transform (possibly log2(FPKM+1); not stated).
- Thresholds and the SSTR1 pre-specification were actually fixed before inspection. This is not verifiable from the packet.
- UniProt annotation reflects localization in these tumors. *Outside knowledge:* DPP4 is primarily a peptidase, so its "membrane receptor" class label deserves checking.

## 5. Alternative explanations

- **For Conclusion C (tier-1 genes):**
  - A shift in tumor phenotype mix (more AR-low or neuroendocrine tumors) in the exposed group (*outside knowledge* for the prevalence claim).
  - Purity differences.
  - Confounding by prior chemotherapy or longer disease course.
  - Residual site effects within site categories.
- **For Conclusion B (Androgen Response down in CRPC):** castration status rather than progression biology, composition not captured by the markers, or autopsy handling.
- **For proliferation-up:** metastasis vs primary, and higher tumor purity in metastases inflating epithelial and cycling genes. The negative-correlation blind spot means this is not checked.
- **For Conclusion A:** some flagged genes may be truly tumor-regulated but also correlated with stroma, for example if stromal and epithelial AR signaling co-vary. The filter could remove real signal.
- **For SSTR1:**
  - test choice (Welch's t-test vs limma)
  - multiple-probe effects
  - weak AR correlation driven by a shared confounder such as purity or lineage
  - a post hoc site pattern

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Computational steps on public data (cheap, do first):**
1. **Refit discovery with a formal model.** Use limma with patient as a blocking factor, if patient IDs are available, and report SSTR1, AR, EZH2 and AURKA.
2. **Extend the composition check:**
   - make the flag two-sided (|rho| ≥ 0.5);
   - report all four signed correlations per gene;
   - refit differential expression with composition and purity scores as covariates across *all* samples;
   - score CRPC samples for site-tissue signatures.
3. **Model AR activity directly in SU2C:**
   - define an AR-activity score in advance;
   - test exposed vs naive on that score;
   - refit tier-1 genes and SSTR1 adjusting for AR score, a neuroendocrine/AR-low score and purity.

   If the tier-1 effects vanish, the signal is a phenotype shift rather than gene-specific regulation.
4. **Test site and expression robustness:**
   - site × ARSI interaction for SSTR1, with per-site n and confidence intervals;
   - detection rates and a count-based model for low-expressed genes;
   - a permutation test of ARSI labels.

**Bench steps (after the reanalysis):**
5. **Perturb AR in vitro.** In an AR-positive prostate cancer model (cell line choice is *outside the material*), run an androgen deprivation/ARSI time course with androgen add-back and AR knockdown. Measure DPP4, TRPM8 and SSTR1 mRNA, and surface protein by flow cytometry.
6. **Check protein in tissue.** If pre/post-ARSI paired tissue is accessible, use immunohistochemistry with validated antibodies. This addresses both the cross-sectional design and the mRNA-vs-protein gap.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

- **Conclusion A:** flagged genes keep the same CRPC-vs-localized difference after composition and purity adjustment, or in tumor-epithelium-specific data.
- **Conclusion B:** the enrichments disappear with a proper array background or ranked analysis, or after purity adjustment.
- **Conclusion C, "AR output" interpretation:**
  - the AR-activity score does not differ between exposed and naive;
  - or adjusting for AR/neuroendocrine scores leaves the tier-1 effects unchanged;

- **Conclusion C, robustness:** effects for SNTG2, TSPAN11 and AGBL2 vanish under count-based or detection-aware models.

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

The material supports three conclusions. Their strength falls from 1 to 3. SSTR1 is not one of them, because its evidence is borderline (see conclusion 3 and section 4).

**Conclusion 1: Many of the strongest CRPC-vs-localized expression differences track sample composition or handling, so ranking by p-value alone is misleading.**
- *Directly demonstrated:*
  - The packet reports that 1,282 of 5,746 hits were flagged (34% of downregulated, 3% of upregulated), including 20 of the top 25.
  - Eight flagged genes are shown with log2FC -3.9 to -6.8, FDR ≤ 1.7e-25 and composition rho 0.57-0.89. Examples are MS4A2 (0.889, mast cell), ACTG2 (0.848, stroma) and PCP4 (0.823, stroma).
- *Interpretation added:*
  - These genes reflect benign stroma, immune cells or ischemia-related handling, not tumor-cell biology.
  - The surviving hits are therefore more likely tumor-intrinsic.
- *Uncertain:*
  - The 20-of-25 figure and the full flagged list are not shown.
  - The rho ≥ 0.5 cutoff is arbitrary. It was computed only across localized tumors, so it cannot capture autopsy-specific effects. How negative correlations were handled is unstated.

**Conclusion 2: After filtering, the CRPC-vs-localized signature is dominated by increased proliferation programs and decreased androgen-response programs.**

- *Interpretation added:*
  - This shows tumor-cell proliferation and loss of AR output, and the biology is "coherent."

- *Uncertain:*

## 3. Reasoning

- Genes whose expression tracks stroma, immune or stress scores within localized tumors are plausibly sampling-dependent. Removing them before pathway analysis makes the remaining signal more likely tumor-intrinsic. This logic is sound but not proven.
- Two independent lines are concordant: androgen-response genes are down in CRPC, and several of them are lower after ARSI. The AR control behaves as expected. This supports a general AR-output story.
- SSTR1 fits that story weakly. The effect shrinks and loses significance after site adjustment, and AR-activity correlation is small (rho 0.19, about 4% of variance). It is compatible with an AR link but does not establish one.

## 5. Alternative explanations

- Differences are driven by the tissue source (metastasis site, autopsy) rather than castration resistance. The SU2C site adjustment partly addresses this, but only for the ARSI contrast.
- Prior therapy, selection of patients who received ARSI, or tumor purity differences explain the ARSI-exposed vs naive differences.
- Low-expressed genes (SNTG2, AGBL2) produce noisy or biased FPKM estimates.
- Genes flagged for composition are partially tumor-expressed. Genes passing the filter may still carry stromal signal (TSPAN11 rho 0.416; several tier-2 genes ≈ 0.43).
- SSTR1 differences are site-specific or small enough to be chance (p = 0.077, FDR 0.334). The AR correlation could reflect shared tumor-cell fraction.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

The working hypothesis is that these genes are tumor-intrinsic and fall with loss of AR output, with SSTR1 as an AR-linked case.
- Flagged genes show tumor-cell expression, or surviving genes show stromal expression.
- Tier-1 changes disappear with limma, with an AR-activity covariate, or in a CRPC-vs-localized replication.
- SSTR1 does not fall (mRNA or surface protein) after AR inhibition or knockdown.
- SSTR1's ARSI effect remains non-significant at larger sample size, or is confined to one site.
- Surface SSTR1 protein is unchanged in CRPC despite lower mRNA.
