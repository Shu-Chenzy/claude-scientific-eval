## Response

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

**Hypothesis (H):** Surface SSTR1 protein is an AR-dependent receptor. It falls when AR signalling is suppressed, so it should be lower in ARSI-exposed metastatic CRPC than in ARSI-naive disease, and an SSTR1-directed ligand therapy is less likely to find a stable target in the ARSI-exposed setting. [I]

**Why this hypothesis over the alternatives:**
- **DPP4 and TRPM8** have the strongest replication (tier 1, FDR < 0.01 in SU2C) [E]. The packet itself says they are androgen-regulated, so their decline mostly restates loss of AR output [E for androgen regulation; I for the interpretation]. Testing their surface status is useful only as a comparator, so I used them as internal benchmarks inside the SSTR1 experiment rather than as the lead hypothesis.
- **Tier 1 genes as targets:** DPP4 is broadly expressed outside the prostate, which argues against tumor selectivity [O]. TRPM8 is a prostate-epithelial gene often reduced in CRPC [O]. Neither is a stated programme target in the packet.
- **Composition-flagged hits** (PAGE4, FCER1A, MS4A2, ACTG2 and others) are stroma or mast-cell artifacts and are excluded [E].
- **Proliferation pathways** (E2F, G2-M) are up in CRPC but are not surface targets [I].
- **SSTR1** is the only candidate that is a cell-surface GPCR, suited to ligand-based therapy, pre-specified, and linked to bench data [E]. The bench result is referenced in the packet but **not provided to me**, so I cannot evaluate it [unverified].

**Recommendation: NEED MORE DATA.** Not a go, because SSTR1 failed the packet's replication threshold. Not a no-go, because the direction is consistent across both cohorts, the signal is clean of composition, and the AR link is plausible.
- **Strongest evidence:** the CRPC decrease in GSE35988 (log2FC −1.18, FDR 0.047, composition rho 0.01) [E].
- **Biggest uncertainty:** whether surface SSTR1 protein tracks mRNA and AR output, and whether the ARSI association survives limma and AR-adjusted modelling [E that the packet flags both issues].
- **Highest decision-value experiment:** Step 1 below, surface SSTR1 by flow under AR suppression with AR-output readouts. It is the only experiment that separates "AR-dependent surface receptor" from "mRNA noise or passenger readout," and it directly governs target viability.

- [E] The ARSI effect is concentrated in lymph node and bone biopsies.

**Indirect evidence**

- [E] The site-adjusted effect is weaker and concentrated at two biopsy sites, which could reflect sampling.

- [E] Only mRNA is measured; surface protein is not.
- [E] AR correlation is weak (rho 0.19) and does not show causation.

## 3. Reasoning

1. The replicated ARSI-associated signal is dominated by AR-output genes [E].
2. SSTR1 is the only pre-specified surface receptor in the candidate set. Its ARSI association is directionally consistent but weak in SU2C [E].
3. The programme decision depends on surface protein, not mRNA [I].
4. So the cheapest informative sequence is: a computational robustness check first, then a single wet-lab test of whether surface SSTR1 depends on AR. Tissue work only follows a positive result.

**Falsifiable prediction:** In an AR-dependent CRPC line (e.g., VCaP [O, example]), 7 days of enzalutamide, or AR siRNA, reduces non-permeabilized surface SSTR1 by ≥40% versus vehicle by flow cytometry with a validated antibody. This reduction should track KLK3 and TMPRSS2 mRNA decline. The same treatment in an AR-null line (e.g., PC-3 [O, example]) should produce no comparable change. The 40% threshold is my judgement, not from the packet.

## 4. Assumptions

- A validated, surface-specific SSTR1 antibody is available.
- The SU2C ARSI association reflects AR-linked biology rather than an artifact.
- The chosen cell lines are reasonable models of AR-dependent CRPC.
- Surface protein is not wholly decoupled from mRNA over the treatment window.
- The packet's numbers and thresholds are correct as stated.
- The bench result exists and is consistent, as the user reports. I have not seen it.

## 5. Alternative explanations

- **Passenger readout:** SSTR1 is one of many AR-output genes and has no independent biology. Predicts that SSTR1 changes track AR readouts and disappear after adjusting for AR score. [I]
- **Biopsy-site or sampling artifact:** Predicts the effect is driven by site composition or tumor content. [I]
- **Chance:** FDR 0.047 in discovery and 0.334 in replication. [E]
- **Unmodelled cell composition:** Neuroendocrine or other non-tumor cells that express SSTR1 may not be captured by the marker scores. [S]
- **Protein persistence:** Surface SSTR1 may be stable despite mRNA loss, so mRNA-only changes would not matter for targeting. [S]
- **Confounding by treatment history:** Duration of ARSI, chemotherapy and disease burden differ between groups. [I]

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Step 0: Computational robustness check (no wet lab)**
- Rerun SSTR1 in GSE35988 and SU2C with limma and site adjustment. Add an AR-activity covariate. Report SSTR1 detection rate in SU2C.
- *Gate to continue:* ARSI effect nominally significant (p < 0.05) under limma with site adjustment, and not fully explained by AR score.
- *Stop programme here if:* the limma site-adjusted effect is null (p > 0.2). Record the AR-adjusted result either way.

**Step 1: Wet-lab decision experiment (surface SSTR1 vs AR suppression)**
- Treat AR-dependent CRPC lines with enzalutamide, vehicle, or AR siRNA. Use an AR-null line as a specificity control.
- Measure non-permeabilized surface SSTR1 by flow at day 0, 3 and 7. Read out KLK3 and TMPRSS2 (AR output) and DPP4 and TRPM8 (AR-dependent benchmarks) by qPCR in parallel.
- *Gate to continue:* ≥40% surface SSTR1 reduction in AR-dependent lines, concordant with KLK3/TMPRSS2, with no comparable change in the AR-null line.
- *Stop programme if:* (a) no surface change despite mRNA change (protein is not AR-dependent); or (b) surface SSTR1 is unchanged while AR readouts fall (H falsified); or (c) the antibody fails specificity controls and no orthogonal surface method is available.

**Step 2: Patient tissue (only if Step 1 passes)**
- Blinded membrane SSTR1 IHC with AR IHC on ARSI-exposed versus ARSI-naive metastases from the same site, with a pathologist scoring tumor content.
- *Go branch:* if membrane SSTR1 is clearly retained in ARSI-exposed tumors at levels similar to naive tumors, H is false in its target-viability sense. That is a go for an ARSI-era SSTR1 programme.
- *Stop branch:* if membrane SSTR1 is clearly reduced in ARSI-exposed tumors at matched sites, a no-go for the ARSI-exposed indication is supported; consider the ARSI-naive setting only.

**Feed-back to the next analysis round (whatever the outcome)**
- Add a flag separating AR-output genes from independent candidates in the tiering rules.
- Record the Step 0 limma and AR-adjusted results for SSTR1.
- Add surface-protein evidence for every candidate, so the table reflects the target-viability question and not only mRNA.
- Extend the composition scores to include neuroendocrine markers. [S]
- Report power or detection rate per gene.
- Record negative and null results with the same weight as hits.
- Enter the bench SSTR1 data into the packet once it can be shared.

## 7. Key controls

- Antibody specificity: SSTR1 knockout or knockdown cells, plus isotype control.
- Non-permeabilized staining for surface readout; permeabilized staining as a total-protein comparison.
- Parallel qPCR for SSTR1 mRNA, so mRNA and protein can be compared directly.
- AR-output readouts (KLK3, TMPRSS2) to confirm AR suppression worked.
- AR-null line (PC-3 [O, example]) under identical treatment.
- Vehicle-matched timepoints.
- Enzalutamide viability control, so loss of signal is not simply cell death.
- Multiple AR-dependent lines, authenticated.
- DPP4 and TRPM8 as AR-dependent benchmarks.
- Blinded IHC scoring with pathologist tumor-content assessment and matched biopsy site.

## 8. What would falsify the hypothesis?

- Surface SSTR1 is unchanged under AR suppression in AR-dependent lines, while AR-output genes fall. [Falsifies H directly.]
- SSTR1 mRNA changes but surface protein does not. [Falsifies the protein-level claim.]
- The reduction occurs equally in the AR-null line. [Points to a drug or culture artifact, not AR dependence.]
- Step 0 shows the ARSI association is null under limma with site adjustment. [Falsifies the public-data signal.]
- Membrane SSTR1 is not reduced at matched sites in ARSI-exposed tissue (Step 2). [Falsifies the clinical implication.]

---

## 9. Uncertainty / limitations

- The bench result is not in the packet, so I cannot assess how strong it is or whether it is independent of the public data.
- The public-data ARSI association is not replicated at the packet's threshold.
- Power is low for a gene at 1.25 log2 FPKM.
- Biopsy site is a likely confound, and only partly modelled.
- Association is not causation. Cell lines may not reflect metastatic biology.
- The composition check is marker-based, not a full deconvolution.
- Welch's t-test was used; limma may change borderline genes.
- The evaluation is small and is meant to find failure modes, not to give a benchmark.

---

## 10. Confidence

**40%** that the recommendation is right: a short computational check followed by one surface-protein experiment, with SSTR1 not yet a go.

For reference, my estimate that H itself is true is about **30–35%**. The main reasons are the failed replication and the low expression. The main reason for not going lower is the consistent direction and the plausible AR link.
