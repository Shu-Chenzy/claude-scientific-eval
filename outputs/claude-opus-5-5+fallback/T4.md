## Response

# Minimal causal test: does loss of a candidate gene drive ARSI resistance?

## 1. Main conclusion / recommendation

The packet supports association only, and the most likely explanation for all six replicated candidates is that they are **passengers** — AR target genes whose expression falls because AR output falls under ARSI pressure (the packet says this explicitly for TRPM8 and DPP4, and the Hallmark Androgen Response signature is the dominant downregulated program). The minimal experiment must therefore test **sufficiency of loss**: knock the gene out in an ARSI-*sensitive*, AR-positive cell line and ask whether that alone shifts enzalutamide sensitivity.

Recommended lead gene: **DPP4** — tier 1, largest and most reproducible effect (log2FC −4.78; SU2C β −1.01, FDR 0.0026), highest expression of the candidates (median log2FPKM 2.33, so detectable protein is plausible), cell-surface, low composition rho (0.165). The identical design applies to SSTR1 if that is the lab's committed line, but note the caveat in §9: SSTR1's own data are weak (SU2C FDR 0.334) and its stated value in the packet is as a *ligand-binding target*, which is a different question from resistance causality and would need a different experiment (surface protein quantification, not knockout).

**One line, four arms, one drug-response endpoint, plus a cDNA rescue. Nothing else until it reads out.**

## 2. Evidence

**Direct evidence (from the packet)**
- DPP4: down in CRPC vs localized (log2FC −4.78, FDR 8.8e-18) and down in ARSI-exposed vs ARSI-naive mCRPC (site-adjusted β −1.01, FDR 0.0026). Not composition-driven (rho 0.165).
- Five other genes replicate in the same direction (ST6GALNAC1, TRPM8, SNTG2, TSPAN11, AGBL2), all down.
- AR positive control behaves correctly (up after ARSI, p = 3e-5), so the replication model is calibrated.
- SSTR1: log2FC −1.18 (FDR 0.047), site-adjusted β −0.34, p = 0.077, FDR 0.334; composition rho 0.0146.

**Indirect evidence**
- Downregulated hits are enriched for Hallmark Androgen Response (41/100, adj p 1.1e-11); the packet's own interpretation is that "the most reproducible signal is loss of AR output under ARSI pressure."
- SSTR1 correlates with AR activity (rho 0.19, p = 0.002) — again consistent with downstream, not upstream.
- Upregulated program is E2F/G2-M — proliferation, not a specific resistance mechanism.

**Evidence absent from the packet:** any perturbation, any protein-level measurement, any ARSI-resistance (as opposed to ARSI-exposure) phenotype.

## 3. Reasoning

Resistance-associated *loss* of expression has three possible causal structures: (a) loss causes resistance; (b) resistance (loss of AR signaling) causes loss — pure passenger; (c) both are driven by lineage change/de-differentiation. Expression data cannot separate these. The only cheap discriminator is perturbation in the sensitive state: if (a), removing the gene from a drug-sensitive cell should move the dose–response curve. If (b) or (c), it should not.

Sufficiency is the right first question because it is a single-arm perturbation and because a negative result is immediately decisive for the "driver" hypothesis. Necessity (re-expression in a resistant line) is the complementary experiment but is a worse first move: resistant lines carry many co-occurring changes, and a negative there is uninterpretable.

Two artifacts can manufacture a false positive, and both are structural to this assay type:
- **Proliferation artifact.** Enzalutamide is largely cytostatic. Any knockout that slows baseline growth reduces the *relative* drug effect and reads out as resistance. This is the single most common false positive in ARSI sensitivity screens and must be controlled by design, not by hope.
- **Cutting/transduction artifact.** Cas9 double-strand breaks alone impose a fitness cost (and more so at amplified loci); selection pressure then picks for cells that edited poorly or grew fast.

## 4. Assumptions

1. The chosen cell line expresses the gene at the protein level at baseline. **This is a gate, not an assumption — verify first (1 day, qPCR + western/flow).** If not expressed, the experiment is void.
2. The line is AR-positive and enzalutamide-growth-inhibited with a usable dynamic window (requires charcoal-stripped serum + low DHT; in full FBS the window is too small to detect a shift).
3. Loss of function is cell-autonomous and detectable in 2D monoculture. For DPP4 specifically this is questionable — it is a shed/soluble ectoenzyme with stromal sources, so monoculture may not reproduce the relevant context.
4. "ARSI exposure" in SU2C is a usable proxy for resistance (the packet flags that it is not).
5. Polyclonal KO pools (not single clones) are adequate — they are, and they avoid clonal bottleneck artifacts.

## 5. Alternative explanations

- **Passenger of AR output loss** (most likely; supported by the Hallmark enrichment and the packet's own text).
- **Lineage/NEPC shift**: AR-pathway genes fall together as tumors de-differentiate; the candidate is a marker of that state.
- **Chemotherapy, metastatic site, or autopsy-vs-surgery collection** differences in the discovery cohort (packet limitation: composition flag removes obvious artifacts, not all).
- **mRNA ≠ protein** — the measured decrease may not correspond to functional protein loss.
- **Statistical fragility**: the packet notes Welch's t-test could move borderline genes such as SSTR1; limma might not retain it.

## 6. Proposed experiment / next step

**Intervention:** CRISPR/Cas9 knockout, polyclonal pools, **two independent sgRNAs** targeting non-overlapping early coding exons. Lentiviral sgRNA into a Cas9-expressing line; puromycin selection; use pools 7–10 days post-selection without clonal expansion.

**Model:** LNCaP-Cas9 (AR-positive, enzalutamide-sensitive, androgen-dependent), grown in 10% charcoal-stripped FBS + 0.1 nM DHT. One line only for the primary test.

**Readout and primary endpoint:**
- 8-point enzalutamide dose–response (0–40 µM), 7–10 days, CellTiter-Glo or confluence imaging.
- Viability normalized **within each genotype to its own vehicle wells**.
- **Primary endpoint: fold-change in GI50 (KO vs non-targeting control), pre-specified threshold ≥3-fold, required concordant for both sgRNAs.** Normalized AUC as the co-primary, since GI50 is unstable if curves do not plateau.
- Secondary: 4-week outgrowth under fixed 10 µM enzalutamide (crystal violet / confluence), run in parallel.

**Arms (6 total):** NT sgRNA; AAVS1/intergenic cutting control; sg1; sg2; sg1-KO + empty vector; sg1-KO + sgRNA-resistant cDNA.

**Mandatory validation before the assay:** editing efficiency (ICE/amplicon sequencing, target >80% indels) and protein loss (western, or surface flow for DPP4/SSTR1). An unvalidated knockout makes a negative result meaningless.

**Mechanism anchor (cheap, include it):** qPCR panel of AR targets (KLK3, TMPRSS2, NKX3-1) ± enzalutamide at one dose. If the KO confers resistance *and* restores AR output under drug, the mechanism is AR-pathway reactivation; if resistance occurs with AR output still suppressed, it is bypass. This costs one plate and determines what you do next.

**Do not do yet:** in vivo castrated xenografts, RNA-seq, the other five genes, single-cell clones, second cell line. None of these change the conclusion of the sufficiency test; all are escalations contingent on a positive.

## 7. Key controls

| Artifact | Control | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Nonspecific/general toxicity | Vehicle arm per genotype; parallel docetaxel (or staurosporine) dose–response | KO shifts enzalutamide GI50 but **not** docetaxel GI50 (<1.5-fold) |
| Proliferation artifact | Baseline growth curve in vehicle, all arms; within-genotype normalization; report absolute cell counts alongside normalized viability | KO vehicle doubling time within ~20% of NT; if slower, the apparent resistance is discounted |
| Cas9 cutting / DNA-damage artifact | AAVS1 or intergenic cutting sgRNA (not just non-targeting) | Cutting control indistinguishable from NT |
| Transduction/selection artifact | Matched MOI and puromycin selection across arms; polyclonal pools; two independent sgRNAs | Both sgRNAs agree; effect size tracks editing efficiency |
| Off-target / passenger-locus effect | sgRNA-resistant cDNA rescue (§ below) | Rescue restores sensitivity |
| Assay sensitivity (that the assay *can* detect resistance) | Positive control arm: AR-overexpressing or AR-F877L/T878A pool, or simply a higher-DHT arm | Produces a clear rightward shift |

## 8. Rescue strategy — and its limits

Re-express the candidate from a constitutive promoter using a cDNA carrying silent mutations in the sgRNA seed/PAM, in the sg1 KO pool; endpoint is restoration of the enzalutamide GI50 toward control. Include an empty-vector KO arm so the rescue comparison is not confounded by the second transduction.

**Establishes:** that the phenotype is on-target for this gene rather than an off-target cut, a passenger effect at the locus, or a clonal artifact.

**Does not establish:** (i) that the physiological expression level matters — constitutive overexpression can overshoot and can rescue by non-physiological mechanisms; (ii) mechanism (for DPP4, catalytic vs scaffold — that requires an enzymatically dead S630A arm, an optional add-on); (iii) relevance in vivo, under true castration, with stroma, or in patients; (iv) that the gene is *necessary* for resistance in patient tumors that already lost it.

## 9. Expected result if the hypothesis is true; falsifying result

**If true:** both sgRNA pools show ≥3-fold higher enzalutamide GI50 (and higher normalized AUC) than NT and cutting controls, concordant across 3 independent transductions; survival/outgrowth at 10 µM over 4 weeks; **no** shift in docetaxel sensitivity; vehicle growth rate unchanged; sgRNA-resistant cDNA restores sensitivity to within 1.5-fold of control.

**Explicit falsification:** with ≥80% editing and confirmed protein loss for both sgRNAs, enzalutamide GI50 and normalized AUC are within 2-fold of NT/cutting controls (95% CIs overlapping) and there is no outgrowth advantage at 4 weeks. That result says loss of the gene is **not sufficient** to cause resistance in this model and reclassifies it as a passenger of AR-output loss — the prior-favored explanation. Partial falsifications that are equally decisive for the specific claim: the shift is present but matched in magnitude for docetaxel (nonspecific drug tolerance); the shift disappears when normalized for baseline proliferation; or the shift is not rescued by the sgRNA-resistant cDNA (off-target).

## 10. Scale and feasibility

- **Replicates:** 3 independent biological replicates = 3 separate transductions/selections, each with technical triplicate wells per dose. Technical replicates within one transduction do not count as n.
- **Power:** n=3 independent transductions reliably detects ~2.5–3-fold GI50 shifts; a 1.5–2-fold shift would need n≥6 and is probably not biologically interesting here anyway — hence the pre-specified 3-fold threshold.
- **Duration:** ~1 week line generation + selection, 1 week editing/protein validation, 10-day dose–response (×3 staggered), 4-week outgrowth in parallel → **6–8 weeks**.
- **Cost/footprint:** 6 arms × 2 drugs × 8 doses × 3 wells ≈ two 96-well plates per replicate. Small.

**What would make this infeasible as described:**
1. **Target not expressed in LNCaP.** The single biggest risk. Both DPP4 and SSTR1 are modestly expressed in tumors (log2FPKM 2.33 and 1.25); if the line lacks detectable protein, the knockout cannot do anything and the whole experiment is void. Run the 1-day expression gate first and be willing to switch lines (VCaP, LAPC4, or a patient-derived organoid).
2. **Inadequate enzalutamide window.** In full serum LNCaP is only weakly inhibited; without androgen-depleted media + titrated DHT there is no room to detect a rightward shift, and the experiment produces an uninterpretable negative.
3. **No reliable antibody** for surface SSTR1 — SSTR antibodies are notoriously poor, and without protein confirmation a negative knockout result is not publishable or trustworthy. (*Outside knowledge: the poor specificity of commercial somatostatin-receptor antibodies is a well-known reagent problem not stated in the packet.*) A radioligand- or tagged-knock-in-based readout may be required, which pushes this out of "minimal."
4. **Non-cell-autonomous biology.** If DPP4's relevant activity is stromal/soluble, monoculture will give a false negative; this would require co-culture or in vivo work, beyond the minimal design.

**Confidence: 72%**
